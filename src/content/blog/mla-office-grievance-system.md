Visit any MLA office on a weekday morning. There is a queue outside, a PA with two phones, and a WhatsApp group where ward coordinators post complaints as photos. A road is broken in ward 4. A water connection is pending. A name is missing from a ration card. Someone wants the MLA at a function on Sunday.

Each request is real, each person expects an answer, and by Thursday most of it has scrolled off the top of the group.

## The cost of a forgotten request

A citizen who raised a complaint and heard nothing does not conclude that the office was busy. They conclude that nobody cared. Multiply that by a few hundred people a month and you have a mood that no amount of campaign work in the last six months will fix.

The office also loses something: it cannot answer basic questions. Which department is slowest? Which ward raises the most complaints? How many requests were closed this month? Without that, the MLA is managing the constituency blind.

## What a grievance system actually needs to do

Not much, and that is the point. The systems that fail are the ones that try to replace how people already behave. Citizens will keep calling and walking in. Ward coordinators will keep using WhatsApp. The system has to sit underneath that.

1. **One place for every request**, whether it came by phone, visit, WhatsApp or the citizen app. The PA enters it in thirty seconds; the citizen can enter it themselves from a link.
2. **An owner and a department for each request.** PWD, water supply, revenue, police, electricity. The request is assigned, not just noted.
3. **A follow-up date** so nothing goes quiet.
4. **A WhatsApp message to the citizen** when the request is received, when it moves and when it closes. This one step changes how the office is perceived.
5. **Ward-wise and department-wise reports** so the MLA knows where the pressure is.

## What it should not do

It should not require citizens to download an app. A link that opens in the browser and logs in with a mobile number is enough. It should not require the office staff to learn a complicated screen; if it takes longer than the WhatsApp group, they will go back to the group.

## A note on data

Citizen complaints contain personal data. Keep the system on a server you control, limit who can export data, and write down how long records are kept. The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 applies to political offices too, and a well-run grievance system is easier to defend than a WhatsApp group with three years of photos in it.

Every lodge, hotel and dharamshala in Maharashtra is required to keep a register of guests and show it to the police on demand. Walk into most lodges in a taluka town and that register is a long notebook, filled in by whoever is at the counter, sometimes with an ID photocopy stapled in, often without.

The register exists for one reason: when something happens, the police need to know who stayed where. And that is exactly when a notebook fails.

## Where the paper register breaks

**Search is impossible.** A constable looking for a name across forty lodges has to visit forty counters and read forty notebooks. By the time the search is done, the person has left the district.

**Entries are incomplete.** A guest arriving at 11 pm is written in as a name and a town. The ID is "to be collected in the morning" and never is.

**Nobody sees it in time.** A night report from a lodge reaches the police station only if the owner is diligent. There is no way for the SP office to know how many guests are in the district tonight.

**It cannot be audited.** Pages go missing. Entries are added after the fact. There is no way to prove when an entry was made.

## What a digital register changes

A digital register is not a computerised notebook. It changes who can see what, and when.

- The lodge enters the guest on a phone in under a minute, with a photo of the ID. The entry is time-stamped and cannot be silently edited.
- The police station and the SP office see the entry the same minute, on a dashboard that covers every lodge in the district.
- Missing IDs, guests on a watch list and unusually long stays are flagged automatically.
- Search works across every lodge by name, ID number, phone or vehicle, in seconds.
- Night reports and monthly registers are generated, not written.

## What it costs a district

Much less than people assume. The lodge needs nothing but the phone it already has. The police station needs a login on an existing computer. The system runs on a server in India that costs a few hundred rupees a month for a district.

The real cost is adoption: lodge owners have to be shown that it is easier than the notebook, and constables have to trust the dashboard. That is why we start with a pilot.

## How a district pilots it

1. Pick one town with ten to fifteen lodges.
2. Give three lodges access and a one-page Marathi guide. No cost, no purchase order.
3. Give the local police station a login.
4. Run it for a month and let the SP office compare the data with what the notebooks would have given.
5. Decide on a district rollout with real numbers.

We have built exactly this system, called Maha-Stay 360, and submitted pilot proposals to district police in Marathwada. If you are a police officer or a lodge owner who wants to see it, the demo takes fifteen minutes.

## The data protection question

Guest data is sensitive and the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 applies. A digital register done properly is safer than paper: data stays on Indian servers, every access is logged, retention is a written rule, and the police department owns the data. A notebook on a counter has none of those protections.

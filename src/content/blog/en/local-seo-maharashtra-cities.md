It is a hot evening in Nashik and the AC has stopped. Someone in the house picks up a phone and types "AC repair near me". Google shows a map and three businesses. The family calls the first one that has a phone number, opening hours and a few recent reviews.

Your workshop may be closer than all three, and older. If Google is not sure what you do, where you are or whether you are still open, you are not on that screen. The call goes to someone else.

## How Google chooses which local businesses to show

Google has described this in general terms. It comes down to three things.

- Relevance: how well your business matches what the person typed.
- Distance: how far you are from the person searching, or from the place named in the search.
- Prominence: how well known the business appears to be, judged from reviews, mentions on other sites and your own website.

You cannot move your shop, so distance is fixed. The other two are in your hands. Most of **local SEO** is making it easy for Google to be sure who you are and what you do, and giving customers reasons to say so in public.

No one can promise you a position, and anyone who does is guessing. If you run Google or Meta campaigns as well, track which enquiries become real customers, which is how we handle [digital marketing](/services/digital-marketing/).

## Keep your name, address and phone the same everywhere

A typical case: a furniture showroom in Kolhapur has one name on the signboard, a slightly different one on its Google Business Profile, an old landline on a directory listing and a family member's mobile number on Facebook. To a customer this is a small confusion. To a search engine it can look like three different businesses, or one that has shut.

Pick **one exact version** of your name, address and phone number, and use it in every place:

- Google Business Profile
- Your website, in the footer and on the contact page
- Facebook and Instagram pages
- Directory and marketplace listings
- Printed bills, visiting cards and the signboard

Use the real name of the business. Do not stuff it with words like "best" or a list of cities. Add opening hours, and update them for Diwali, Ganeshotsav and other holidays. ## Give each service and each area its own page

Many small business sites have a single "Services" page with ten bullet points. Someone searching for "modular kitchen in Pune" is better served by a page that is only about modular kitchens: what you make, the materials, how pricing works, photos of finished jobs and how to book a visit.

So make one page for each main service. If you work across several cities, say Pune, Pimpri-Chinchwad and Satara, make a page for each area you really serve.

The mistake is copying one page and changing only the city name. Readers notice, and search engines usually treat such pages as thin. Each area page needs something of its own:

1. Work you have actually done there, with photos.
2. How you serve that area: visit days, delivery charges, the nearest branch.
3. Questions that customers from that area usually ask.

## Ask for reviews and reply to every one

Reviews help a customer choose between three names on a map, and they are one of the signals of prominence.

The best moment to ask is right after a job goes well. Send the customer your review link on WhatsApp with a one-line request. Ask them to mention the work done, because "Sofa re-upholstered and delivered on the promised day" tells the next reader more than "Nice service".

Then **reply to every review**. Thank the happy ones by name. For an unhappy one, stay calm, do not argue in public, say what you will do and give a number to call. Future customers read your reply as carefully as the complaint.

Do not buy reviews, write your own or offer discounts in exchange. Review platforms have rules against this and can remove reviews or restrict a profile. Read the platform's current policy before you start a review drive.

## Write local content in Marathi

Many customers in Nagpur, Solapur or Jalgaon search the way they speak. Some type in Devanagari, some type Marathi in English letters, and many speak into the phone. If your site is only in English, nothing on it matches those words.

Add Marathi versions of your main service pages, written fresh by someone who speaks the language, not translated line by line. Then add content that only a business in your city could write: answers to questions you hear at the counter, a seasonal note before the monsoon or the wedding season, a short write-up of a recent job.

## Common questions

### Do I need a website if I already have a Google Business Profile?

Yes, in most cases you need both. The profile puts you on the map with your hours, phone number and reviews, while the website holds the detail on each service and area that helps search engines understand what you do.

### Can I show up in searches from parts of the city far from my shop?

Sometimes, but not reliably. Distance is one of the things local search weighs, so a business closer to the searcher often appears first. Area pages with real work and reviews from customers there improve your chances without guaranteeing them.

### Is it all right to ask customers for reviews?

Yes, you can ask any genuine customer for an honest review. Do not pay for reviews, reward them or write them yourself. If you are unsure, check the review platform's current policy.

A mother in Nashik has a child with fever at nine at night. She types "child doctor near me" into her phone. Google shows a map and a short list of clinics, each with a rating, timings and a call button. She calls the one that says it is open.

Your clinic may be closer to her than the one she called. But if your listing shows no timings, an old landline number and no photo, she has nothing to go on. She did not reject you. She never saw you.

## Why patients choose from the map results

A person searching for a doctor is usually unwell or worried, and on a phone. They want three answers: who is close, who is open now, and who can be trusted.

The map results answer all three on one screen, before any website opens. Typically the patient decides at that point and does not scroll further.

What they see comes from your **Google Business Profile**, a free listing that you claim, verify and manage yourself. If you never claim it, a listing may still exist, built from whatever Google has collected, and that is what patients will read.

## Get the basics right first

Five fields do most of the work.

1. Name. Use the name on your signboard, exactly, with no extra words like "best" or the city name.
2. Category. Choose the primary category closest to your speciality, such as paediatrician, dentist or orthopaedic clinic. A specific category helps Google match you to specific searches.
3. Timings. Enter morning and evening OPD separately, mark the weekly off, and set special hours for festivals such as Diwali. A patient who reaches a closed shutter may not come back.
4. Phone. Give the number that reception answers during OPD hours, not the doctor's personal mobile.
5. Booking link. If you take appointments online, add the link. Our guide on [what a clinic should look for in a doctor appointment booking app](/blog/doctor-appointment-booking-app/) can help you choose.

## Photos of the entrance and waiting area

A family travelling from another district to see a specialist in Kolhapur has never seen your building. A daylight photo of the entrance, with the signboard readable, tells them they have reached the right place.

Useful photos:

- The entrance and signboard, as seen from the road.
- The staircase, lift or ramp, for families bringing elderly patients.
- The reception counter and the waiting area.
- Parking space, if you have it.

Use **real photos of your own clinic**, not stock images. Keep patients out of the frame, and make sure no case paper, prescription or screen with patient details is visible.

## Reviews: ask politely, reply to every one, offer nothing

The best moment to ask is when a patient thanks you. The receptionist can say, "If the visit helped, a review on Google will help other patients find us." No bulk reminders, no reviews from staff or relatives.

**Never offer anything in return**: no discount, no free follow-up, no gift. Google's review policies do not allow incentives. Doctors also work under professional conduct rules on how a practice may be promoted, so confirm what applies with your state medical council or a legal advisor.

Reply to every review. For a good one, a short thank you is enough. For a bad one, stay calm, and do not confirm or reveal anything about the person's visit, diagnosis or treatment. Invite them to call the clinic. Your reply is written as much for the next patient as for the reviewer.

## Posts and answers in Marathi and English

Posts are short updates that appear on your profile. Use them for what a patient would want to know before leaving home: a change in OPD timings, a vaccination day, the dates of a visiting specialist, or how your [OPD tokens on WhatsApp](/blog/opd-tokens-on-whatsapp/) work.

Write each post in both languages. Patients in Latur or Amravati may search in Marathi, in English, or in a mix. A Marathi line followed by the same line in English serves all of them.

If your profile shows a questions and answers section, anyone can ask and anyone can answer. So answer first, yourself: parking, Sunday availability, whether an appointment is needed, which reports to carry. If the section does not appear, put the same answers in posts and on your website. Stick to facts, with no promise of cure.

## Link the profile to your clinic website

The profile has room for the essentials only. The website carries the rest: the doctor's qualifications, the conditions you treat, directions in Marathi and English, and the booking page.

Add the website address to the profile, and put a map and the profile link on the website's contact page. Then check that both show the **same name, address and phone number**. Matching details help Google treat the two as one clinic.

The website also matters for a newer habit: patients asking an AI assistant to suggest a doctor. Our post on [whether your business is visible in AI search](/blog/website-visible-in-ai-search/) explains what those assistants look for.

## Common questions

### Is a Google Business Profile free for a clinic?

Yes, Google does not charge for creating or managing a profile. You need a Google account, and Google will ask you to verify the clinic. Paid advertising is separate and is not required for the listing.

### I consult at two clinics. Do I need two profiles?

Usually yes, each location with its own address and timings gets its own profile. Google has separate guidelines for individual practitioners, so read them before creating the second one.

### Do I need a website if I already have a profile?

No, the profile works without a website. But a website holds the details patients look for next, and gives Google more to match against searches. A simple bilingual site with the same contact details is enough to begin.

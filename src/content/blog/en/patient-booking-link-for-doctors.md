It is Monday morning at a skin clinic in Latur. Six patients are standing at the reception desk, and the phone has not stopped ringing since the shutter went up. The receptionist is writing a name in the appointment diary with one hand and holding the receiver with the other.

Every caller asks the same things: is the doctor in today, what time should I come, how long is the wait. Some callers get an engaged tone and give up. Nobody at the clinic knows how many.

## What phone booking really costs a clinic

The phone feels free because the clinic already has one. The cost shows up elsewhere.

- Receptionist time: every call is a small conversation. Name, complaint, preferred time, a search through the diary, directions to the clinic. While it goes on, the patient at the desk waits to pay or collect a report.
- Missed calls: a patient who rings during the rush, at lunch or after closing gets no answer. Some try again. Some call the next doctor in the Google results.
- Double bookings: two staff members write in the same diary on different days, a line gets scratched out, and two patients arrive for the same time. The doctor hears about it in the consulting room.

None of this is the receptionist's fault. **One person cannot run a desk and a call centre at once.**

## One link, shared where patients already look

A booking link is a web address for your clinic's appointment page. The patient taps it and sees your name, your clinic and the times you are available. It only works if patients find it without asking, so put it in the three places they already look.

- WhatsApp: add it to the clinic's WhatsApp profile and send it as the reply to every "appointment?" message. Patients forward it to relatives on their own.
- Google profile: add it to the clinic's listing, so someone [searching for an ENT doctor](/blog/google-business-profile-for-doctors/) in Kolhapur late at night can book instead of calling a number nobody will answer till morning.
- Visiting card and prescription pad: print it as a QR code. A patient due for a follow-up scans the card at home.

The phone number stays on all three. The link becomes the first option and the call becomes the fallback.

## What the patient does after tapping the link

On a well-built booking page, the steps are short.

1. The patient picks a day and sees only the slots that are still open.
2. They choose a slot and enter a name and mobile number.
3. A confirmation arrives on WhatsApp with the date, time and clinic address.
4. A reminder arrives before the appointment, with a way to cancel so the slot goes back to someone else.

The reminder matters more than it looks. A typical no-show is not a careless patient. It is a forgotten date, and a WhatsApp message does the remembering. If you want these messages to go from an official business number instead of a staff member's personal phone, that is the job of [WhatsApp automation](/services/whatsapp-automation/) on the official Meta Cloud API.

Some systems go a step further and give the patient a [token with queue updates](/blog/opd-tokens-on-whatsapp/), so they leave home when their turn is near instead of filling the waiting room.

## The doctor decides the slots, leave days and limits

The common fear is that a link lets patients fill the day without asking. It works the other way. A paper diary is controlled by whoever picks up the phone. **A booking page shows only what the doctor has opened.**

- Slots: set OPD hours for each day and the length of a consultation. If you sit at your clinic in the morning and at a hospital in the evening, the page shows exactly that.
- Leave days: mark a conference, a surgery day or a festival as closed and those dates stop appearing. Nobody has to call to find out.
- Limits: cap the bookings for each session and hold back places for walk-ins and emergencies.

## Older patients and the family members who book for them

The second objection is: my patients are elderly, they will not use a link. Often true, and often beside the point.

In a typical family, the 70-year-old patient in Amravati is not the one who books. A daughter in Pune or a son in Nashik does it. Today that means calling the clinic from the office during OPD hours and hoping the line is free. With a link, they book at night after work, type the parent's name and forward the confirmation to the family group.

So [check three things](/blog/doctor-appointment-booking-app/) on any booking page:

- One mobile number can book for more than one person, with the patient's name asked for separately.
- The page reads well in Marathi as well as English.
- The receptionist can enter a phone booking on the same page, so patients without a smartphone are still in the one diary.

## Common questions

### Should the clinic stop taking appointment calls?

No, keep the phone for patients who prefer it and for emergencies. The aim is fewer routine calls, so the calls that do come get answered.

### What happens to walk-in patients?

They are seen as before. The receptionist adds them to the same list, and the limit you set on online bookings keeps places free for them.

### What if the doctor is running late or is called to an emergency?

The clinic closes the remaining slots and informs booked patients by message, instead of making a round of phone calls. Ask any provider to show you this exact situation in the demo before you decide.

It is 9 pm at a 25-bed hospital in Latur. A patient is ready for discharge and the family is waiting at the counter. The receptionist is adding up slips: room charges, doctor's visits, medicines, two lab tests. The deposit paid on admission is written on the back of the file.

Some weeks later the accountant asks how much of that total was treatment and how much was medicine sold across the counter. Nobody can say. Most billing and GST trouble in a small hospital starts there.

## Keep four kinds of bills separate

A hospital earns money in four ways, and each behaves differently in the accounts. Give each its own bill type and number series, even when the patient pays once at the counter.

- Consultation: the doctor's fee, with the doctor's name and the date.
- IPD: room, nursing, doctor's visits, procedures and theatre charges, one line each.
- Pharmacy: every medicine with batch, expiry, quantity and the tax rate for that item.
- Lab: each test by name, with the doctor who ordered it.

The patient can still receive **one summary at discharge**. Behind that summary the four heads stay separate, so the accountant can total each one without opening a patient file.

Pharmacy bills also reduce stock as they are made, which is why [pharmacy stock and expiry tracking](/blog/pharmacy-stock-expiry-hospital-software/) depends on clean pharmacy billing.

## How GST generally treats services and medicines

In general terms, GST treats healthcare services and goods differently. Treatment given by a hospital, clinic or doctor is generally exempt. Medicines, consumables and other items sold over the pharmacy counter are generally taxable, and the rate depends on the item.

The difficult cases sit between the two:

- Medicines and consumables used during an admission, as part of treatment, are often treated differently from a counter sale to a walk-in customer.
- Some room charges, and some procedures that are not treatment of an illness, may not get the same exemption.
- Whether the hospital or its pharmacy needs a GST registration depends on its own sales.

Rates and exemption conditions are revised by the government from time to time, so this post quotes no rate and no limit.

What the hospital controls is the record. Every bill line should carry a marker: service or goods, exempt or taxable, and the rate applied. **Your CA decides the treatment**, and the software applies it the same way on every bill, whoever is at the counter.

## Package bills and deposits

Many small hospitals quote a package: one price for a delivery or a planned surgery. It is simple for the family and risky for the accounts unless three things are written down.

1. What the package includes: room days, theatre, doctor's fees, medicines, tests.
2. What counts as extra: additional room days, medicines outside the list, a second procedure.
3. How medicines used inside the package are issued, so that pharmacy stock still tallies.

Extras go on the bill as separate lines, never as a handwritten addition to the package amount. Room days should come from the bed record, which is one more reason to keep [a bed board the whole staff can see](/blog/ipd-bed-management-small-hospital/).

Deposits need the same discipline. Each deposit gets its own numbered receipt and stays **an advance, not a sale**, until the final bill is made. The final bill shows the total, the deposits adjusted, and the balance to collect or refund. A refund carries the name of the person who approved it.

## Daily collection and outstanding reports

An owner in Jalgaon or Kolhapur should not have to wait for the month end to know where the money is. Every evening, one phone screen should answer five questions.

1. How much was collected by cash, UPI and card, and does the cash match the drawer?
2. How much came from OPD, IPD, pharmacy and lab?
3. Which deposits were received and which refunds were paid?
4. Which bills were discounted or cancelled, and who approved each one?
5. What is outstanding: admitted patients whose running bill has crossed their deposit, discharged patients with dues, and claims pending with insurers?

At month end the same data should reach the accountant as an export, not a bundle of printouts. ## Confirm rates and exemptions with your CA

Software can apply a rule the same way every time. It cannot decide the rule. Before billing starts on any new system, settle these points with your CA in writing:

- Which of your charges are exempt healthcare services and which are taxable.
- The tax rate and code for each item in the pharmacy master.
- How medicines given to admitted patients, room charges, packages and non-treatment procedures are to be billed.
- Whether the hospital and the pharmacy need registration.

Go through the list again whenever the government announces a change. A hospital with separated bills can adopt a new rate by editing one master.

## Common questions

### Does a small hospital charge GST on every bill?

Generally no. Healthcare services are generally exempt, while pharmacy counter sales and a few other charges are generally taxable. Ask your CA to confirm which of your own charges fall on each side.

### Can the patient get one bill instead of four?

Yes, the patient can receive one discharge summary that lists everything. The consultation, IPD, pharmacy and lab entries should still be stored as separate bill types behind it.

### Is a deposit income on the day it is received?

No, in the hospital's daily records it is better shown as an advance until the final bill adjusts it. How advances are treated for tax is a question for your CA. Either way, every deposit and every refund needs its own numbered receipt.

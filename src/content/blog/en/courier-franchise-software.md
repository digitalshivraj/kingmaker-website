A customer in Nashik calls your head office in Pune to ask where her parcel is. It was booked at your Jalgaon franchise three days ago. Your staff call Jalgaon, Jalgaon checks a receipt book, and then someone calls the hub in Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar. The customer waits on the line and still nobody can say for certain where the parcel is.

This is what a courier network looks like when each franchise books on its own. The parcels move, but the information does not move with them.

## What happens when each franchise books on its own

In a typical network, one branch writes a receipt book, another uses a billing program on one computer, and a third sends an Excel sheet on WhatsApp at night. Each method works for that branch. It fails at head office, where the owner needs all of them together.

The usual problems:

- Consignment numbers repeat, because each branch prints its own series.
- Head office learns of a booking only when the parcel reaches the hub.
- One branch still charges from an old rate card.
- A missing parcel becomes an argument between two branches, with no record of who held it last.
- Commission is worked out from sheets the franchise prepared itself.

Nobody has to be dishonest for this to happen. It is what a network looks like with **no common record**.

## One booking system, one series of consignment numbers

The fix starts at the counter. Every branch books on the same screen, on a phone or the counter computer, and the system issues **one consignment number** from a single series for the whole network.

A booking captures the sender, the receiver, the destination pin code, the weight, the charge and the payment type: paid, to-pay or credit. The rate comes from the rate card head office maintains, so a franchise in Latur and a franchise in Kolhapur quote the same price for the same parcel. The branch prints a receipt and a label carrying the number as a barcode.

Head office sees the booking as soon as it is saved. Nobody waits for a sheet at night.

## Hub scans and movement between branches

A consignment number is only useful if it is scanned every time the parcel changes hands. A workable scan trail runs in this order:

1. Booked at the origin branch.
2. Bagged and dispatched to the hub on a manifest.
3. Received at the hub. Anything on the manifest that is not scanned shows as short.
4. Dispatched from the hub to the destination branch.
5. Received at the destination branch.
6. Out for delivery with a named delivery person.
7. Delivered, with the receiver's name, or returned with a reason.

Each scan records who scanned, at which branch and when. When a parcel goes missing between Nagpur and Amravati, the system shows **the last scan**, and the conversation starts from a fact.

For the owner, the same scans add up to one screen: bookings by branch, parcels sitting at the hub, and deliveries pending at each destination.

A small franchise does not need special hardware. A phone camera can read the barcode, which is why we build [field apps that work on cheap phones and weak networks](/services/mobile-apps/).

## Franchise commissions and settlements

Settlement is where many networks lose goodwill. The franchise brings its figures, head office brings its own, and the two do not match.

When every booking and delivery sits in one system, commission is calculated from a record both sides can see. Head office sets the rules: a share for booking, a share for delivery, and different rates for different franchises if the agreement says so. For each period, the system prepares a statement per franchise:

- Consignments booked and delivered.
- To-pay and cash on delivery amounts collected.
- Commission earned.
- The net amount payable, in either direction.

The franchise sees **the same statement** in its own login. A dispute is then about one consignment, not the whole month.

## Customer tracking by consignment number

Once scans exist, tracking is simply showing them to the customer. She types the consignment number on your website, or sends it to your WhatsApp number, and sees the trail: booked, at hub, out for delivery, delivered.

The customer sees the status and the city. She does not see staff names or internal remarks. The caller from Nashik gets her answer without anyone phoning three offices, and your branches should take fewer "where is my parcel" calls.

If updates go out on WhatsApp, send them through the official API and not from a personal number on a branch phone. Our post on [WhatsApp done the official way](/blog/whatsapp-business-api-vs-tools/) explains the difference.

## Common questions

### Can a franchise keep its own billing software?

Yes, for its own accounts, but every consignment must still be booked in the common system. Otherwise the parcel has no network number, no scans and no place in the settlement.

### Do branches need barcode scanners and computers?

No, a phone with a camera is enough to book and scan. A busy hub may prefer a handheld scanner and a label printer because of volume, but a small franchise can start with the phone it already has.

### Who can see each franchise's data?

Head office sees the whole network, and each franchise sees only its own bookings, scans and statements. This is set by role-based access, so a branch in Solapur cannot open the figures of a branch in Pune. Write the same rule into your franchise agreement.

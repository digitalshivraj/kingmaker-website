At ten in the morning, the reception desk of a 25-bed hospital in Jalgaon has one phone and one receptionist. The phone rings while she is writing a receipt. A caller wants to know if the orthopaedic doctor is sitting today. Before she finishes answering, three patients are at the counter and the phone is ringing again.

Most of those calls have the same few answers. A WhatsApp chatbot can give those answers on the hospital's own number, in Marathi, while the receptionist attends to the people in front of her.

## The calls a reception desk gets every day

Listen to the reception phone for one morning in any small hospital and the questions repeat. Typically they are:

- What time does OPD start, and is it open on Sunday?
- Is the doctor in today, or on leave?
- Where exactly is the hospital, and is there parking?
- Can I get an appointment for tomorrow?
- Is my report ready?
- When is my follow-up, or my child's next vaccine?

None of these needs a trained person, but each one holds the line while a caller with a real problem gets a busy tone. A chatbot does not replace the receptionist. It **frees the phone line** for the calls that need her.

## What the chatbot answers on its own

The patient sends "Hi" to the hospital's WhatsApp number and gets a short menu. From there, **four things can run without staff**.

1. Timings: OPD hours by department, visiting hours, lab sample collection hours and holiday closures.
2. Doctor availability: which doctor is sitting today and when the visiting specialist comes. Staff mark a leave once and every patient who asks gets the right answer.
3. Directions: a location pin that opens in the patient's maps app, with a landmark in words.
4. Booking: the patient picks a doctor and a slot, and receives a token number on the same chat.

Booking is usually where the desk gets the most relief. Our post on [OPD tokens on WhatsApp](/blog/opd-tokens-on-whatsapp/) explains how the queue works when patients wait at home instead of in the corridor.

## Reports go to the registered number only

"Is my report ready?" is the call the lab dreads, because the honest answer is often "let me check". When the chatbot is connected to the lab, the patient need not ask. Once the result is entered and approved, the report goes as a PDF to the mobile number recorded at registration.

The rule to hold firmly is **the registered number only**. A lab report is sensitive personal information. If someone messages from a different number and asks for a patient's report, the chatbot should not send it. It should direct them to the reception, where staff can verify the person and update the number on record.

Hospitals also carry legal duties around patient data. Confirm what applies to yours with a legal advisor.

## Reminders for follow-ups and vaccinations

A patient in Solapur is told to come back next month with a sugar report. Often the patient forgets, and nobody at the hospital notices.

When the doctor sets a follow-up date in the system, the chatbot can send a reminder a day or two before, with a button to book the slot. The same works in a paediatric clinic: the date of the child's next vaccine, as per the schedule the paediatrician follows, goes to the parent's phone.

Two limits keep this respectful. Reminders go **only to patients who have agreed** to receive them, and each one lets the patient stop further messages with a single reply. The wording stays plain: a date, a doctor's name, a way to book.

## When to hand over to a person

A chatbot that tries to answer everything is worse than no chatbot. Decide in advance where it stops.

- Emergencies: if a message mentions chest pain, bleeding, an accident or breathlessness, the chatbot shows the emergency phone number at once and alerts the desk.
- Medical questions: "Can I take this tablet with that one?" goes to a doctor or nurse, never to an automated reply.
- Money and insurance: bill disputes, estimates and cashless claims need a person who can look at the file.
- Confusion: if the chatbot fails to understand the patient twice, or the patient types "staff", the chat moves to the reception.

The handover should happen **on the same chat**. The staff member sees what the patient already asked and replies from the hospital's number.

## Common questions

### Does the chatbot give medical advice?

No. It answers administrative questions such as timings, bookings and report status, and passes anything clinical to a doctor or nurse.

### Do we need hospital software before starting a chatbot?

Not for the basic menu: timings, doctor availability, directions and booking can run on their own. Report delivery and follow-up reminders need lab and OPD records in a system, such as our [hospital management system](/products/hospital-management-system/), which is demo ready and onboarding small hospitals and clinics.

### What about patients who do not use WhatsApp?

They keep calling, and the phone line stays exactly as it is. Because routine questions move to the chatbot, a caller is more likely to find the line free.

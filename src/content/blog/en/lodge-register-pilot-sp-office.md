A constable from a police station in Nanded, Nashik or Kolhapur walks into a lodge near the bus stand and asks for the guest register. He turns pages of hurried handwriting, copies a few names into his diary and moves to the next lodge. By the time his round is over, last night's guests have checked out.

Most SP offices already know the paper register is weak. What is less clear is how to test a digital one without committing the whole district. The answer is a small pilot with a fixed scope, written duties and a plain way to judge it.

## What a pilot covers

Keep the pilot to **one police station limit**. Choose a station with a fair mix: lodges near the railway station and bus stand, a few mid-size hotels, and some properties that receive foreign guests.

The pilot has three parts, in this order.

1. Every lodge and hotel in the limit is listed and given a login.
2. The front desk enters each guest at check-in on a phone or computer.
3. The station checks the entries daily from the station itself, and beat staff visit only where something needs a second look.

Lodges are generally required to keep a guest register and to report foreign guests. Whether a digital entry can replace the paper one, or must sit beside it during the pilot, is for the SP office to confirm with the department or its legal advisor before the start. ## Who does what

A pilot fails when everyone assumes someone else is checking. Write the duties into the pilot order.

- Lodge owner: registers the property, names the staff who will make entries and is answerable when entries are missing.
- Front desk: enters each guest at check-in with ID details and marks the check-out.
- Beat staff: stop copying registers by hand. They visit lodges that show no entries or incomplete ones, and verify on the spot.
- Station in-charge: opens the station view daily, notes which lodges are silent and assigns the follow-up.
- SP office or district special branch: reviews the pilot view, maintains the watch-list and decides what happens when an alert appears.

Name **one officer in the SP office** who owns the pilot, so lodge owners and station staff know whom to call.

## What the SP sees each morning

The purpose is that the SP does not wait for a weekly return. Each morning, **one screen should answer three questions** without a phone call to the station.

- Guest counts: how many guests stayed in the pilot limit last night, lodge by lodge, and which lodges reported none.
- Foreign national entries: which foreign guests are staying, at which property, in one list.
- Watch-list alerts: any guest name that matches a name the police have placed on the watch-list.

An alert is a prompt, not a conclusion. Names repeat, and spellings vary between Marathi and English. A person must verify a match before any action is taken. Decide in advance who receives the alert, who verifies it and where the result is recorded.

A lodge beside a busy bus stand that reports no guests for days is also information. The paper register never showed it.

## Why lodge owners cooperate

A lodge owner cares about two things: less work at the desk and less trouble from the station. The pilot has to deliver both.

Start with the desk. If the entry is **quicker than writing a full line in the paper register**, the front desk will use it. Test this at a lodge, in front of the owner: one guest in the register, the next in the app, same kind of ID. If the app is slower, fix the form before going further.

Then the station. When the station can see entries from its own desk, a lodge that enters every guest properly has less reason to be visited. Say this plainly at the first meeting with owners, held in Marathi.

The usual objections are weak network, staff who change often and [worry about guests' data](/blog/dpdp-act-government-software/). An officer should answer them, not the software vendor alone.

## How to judge the pilot before scaling to the district

Fix the review date and the measures before the first entry is made. These questions are enough.

1. Coverage: how many listed lodges make entries every day, and which have stopped?
2. Accuracy: on surprise visits, do the digital entries match the guests actually in the rooms?
3. Desk speed: does the front desk still find it quicker than paper?
4. Station use: does the station in-charge open the view daily, or only when reminded?
5. Alerts: was each alert verified and recorded as decided, and were there too many false matches?
6. Beat time: are visits now targeted at problem lodges?

If the answers hold up, extend station by station rather than to the whole district at once. ## Common questions

### Does the digital register replace the paper register during the pilot?

Not unless the department confirms that it can. A sensible approach is to run both side by side in the pilot limit and compare them. Confirm the position with the department or a legal advisor before any lodge stops the paper register.

### What does a lodge need to take part?

A phone or computer at the front desk, and one staff member who has been shown how to make entries. The owner registers the property and adds the staff. A small lodge with one person at the desk takes part the same way as a larger hotel.

### Who can see the guest data?

Only people whose role requires it. The lodge sees its own guests, the station sees its limit and the SP office sees the pilot view. Guest details are personal data, so confirm the data protection duties with a legal advisor.

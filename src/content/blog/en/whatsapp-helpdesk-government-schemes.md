At the scheme desk of a district office in Jalgaon, Latur or Amravati, the day starts the same way. The phone rings, a queue forms at the counter, and the clerk answers what she answered yesterday: am I eligible, which papers do I bring, where has my application reached, which office do I go to.

None of these questions needs an officer's judgement. Yet each one can cost a citizen a bus fare and a day's wage, and keeps the clerk away from the files. A WhatsApp helpdesk in Marathi takes the repeated questions off the counter and leaves the staff for cases that need a person.

## The four questions every scheme desk hears

Stand at any counter, whether for an agriculture scheme, a housing scheme or a scholarship, and the questions fall into four groups.

- Eligibility: "Can I apply?" This usually turns on land, income, category, age, or whether the family has already received the benefit.
- Documents: "What do I bring?" And are originals needed, or photocopies?
- Status: "Where has my file reached?"
- Office: "Which office, which table, what timings?"

The answers already exist in the scheme guidelines and the office's own register. The trouble is that they sit on a notice board and in the staff's memory, not on the citizen's phone.

## A Marathi menu that answers them

The citizen sends "Namaskar" to the office number or scans a QR code pasted at the counter. The helpdesk replies with a short menu in Marathi, with English on request. In English, the menu reads:

1. Scheme eligibility
2. Documents needed
3. Application status
4. Office address and timings
5. Talk to staff

The citizen replies with a number, so nobody has to type in Devanagari. Option 1 asks which scheme and shows the published conditions in simple sentences. Option 2 sends a checklist the citizen can carry to the photocopy shop. Option 4 sends the address, a map location, the timings and the name of the desk.

**Every answer uses wording the office has approved.** A scheme helpdesk should not improvise. When guidelines change, an authorised staff member edits the text and the next citizen gets the corrected answer. ## Application status, where the data is available

Status is usually the most asked question, and a helpdesk can report only what the office can supply. Typically an office is in one of three situations.

- The office keeps its own application register in a spreadsheet or software. The helpdesk reads from it. The citizen sends the application number and gets the stage in plain Marathi: received, under scrutiny, query raised, sanctioned, payment sent.
- The status sits on a state portal the office does not control. The helpdesk does not guess. It sends the official link and the steps to check there.
- There is no digital record. The status option hands over to staff, and the office learns which register to digitise first.

One safeguard matters here. **A status is personal information**, so the helpdesk shows it only when the WhatsApp number matches the mobile number on the application, or after the citizen confirms a second detail.

## Handover to a staff member for everything else

No menu covers a farmer whose name is spelt differently on two documents, or an applicant whose bank account has changed. Option 5, and any message the helpdesk cannot place, goes to a staff inbox.

A good handover has four parts.

- The staff member sees the full conversation, so the citizen does not repeat the story.
- The citizen is told that a person will reply during office hours, and what those hours are.
- Each conversation is assigned to a named staff member.
- The office head sees how many conversations are open and how many are closed.

**The decision stays with the officer.** The helpdesk never says yes or no on a sanction.

## Official business number, opt-in and data handling

Run the helpdesk on the official WhatsApp Business platform, on a number registered in the office's name. On a clerk's personal phone, the chat history leaves when the clerk is transferred. Unofficial bulk tools put the number at risk of a ban. We explain the difference in [WhatsApp for your office or clinic, done the official way](/blog/whatsapp-business-api-vs-tools/).

**Opt-in comes first.** The citizen starts the chat, or agrees on the application form to receive updates on WhatsApp. Messages the office sends first need that consent and a message format approved by Meta. Numbers collected for one scheme should not receive unrelated announcements.

For data, set four rules at the start.

- Ask for the minimum: an application number, not identity numbers or document photos, unless a step needs them.
- Give each staff member access by role.
- Host the data in India and keep a log of who viewed what.
- Decide in writing how long conversations are kept.

How data protection law applies to your office is a question for the department's legal advisor. Our plain-language note on [the DPDP Act for offices buying software](/blog/dpdp-act-government-software/) is a useful starting point.

## Common questions

### Does a citizen need to install a new app?

No, it works inside the ordinary WhatsApp the citizen already uses. They save the office number or scan the QR code at the counter, then send a greeting.

### Can the helpdesk decide who is eligible?

No, it only shows the published conditions and the current stage of an application. Sanction and rejection remain the officer's decision, and doubtful cases are handed to staff.

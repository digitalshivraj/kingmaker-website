Picture an office manager at a trading company in Nashik who has just hired nine people for a new branch. She asks each of them for an Aadhaar card, PAN card, bank details and a passport photo. Over the following weeks a blurred Aadhaar photo arrives on her personal WhatsApp, a PAN card comes with one corner cropped, a passbook page goes to the owner instead of her, and two people keep saying "sending today".

When payroll has to run, the documents sit on three different phones and nobody can say with confidence who has given what. The work is not difficult. It is scattered, and it stays scattered long after the employee has joined.

## Why the chase happens

Personal chat is the easiest place to send a photo, so that is where everything lands. The trouble starts after it lands.

- Files arrive in pieces, mixed in with leave requests and good morning messages.
- Photos are taken in poor light, at an angle, or with a thumb over the number.
- The same document goes to different people: the manager, the owner, the accountant.
- Nobody has one list of what is pending, so reminders go out from memory.
- When a staff member leaves the company, the documents on their phone leave with them.

None of this is anyone's fault. A chat app is made for conversation, not for keeping records. The fix is to keep WhatsApp for what it does well, which is reaching the person, and move the collecting to a place made for it.

## One link per employee, opened on the phone

Instead of asking for photos in a chat, the office sends each new employee **one personal link** on WhatsApp. They tap it and a simple page opens in the phone's browser, in Marathi or English, with their name already on it.

The page asks for each item in order:

1. Basic details: full name as on the bank account, date of birth, mobile number and address.
2. Aadhaar card, front and back.
3. PAN card.
4. Bank details, with a photo of a cancelled cheque or the first page of the passbook.
5. A passport-size photo, taken with the phone camera or picked from the gallery.

There is nothing to install and no password to remember. The employee can stop halfway and return on the same link later, for example after finding the passbook at home. Because the link belongs to one person, every upload lands in that person's record and nowhere else.

## A checklist that shows what is missing

On the office side, the manager sees one screen with a row for every employee and a column for every document. Each cell shows one of three states: received, pending or **needs a retake**.

This changes the daily work in three ways:

- A reminder goes only to the person who owes something, and it names the item, such as "Your PAN card photo is pending."
- A blurred upload is sent back with a short reason, without a long back-and-forth.
- The owner can open the same screen and see where a branch in Kolhapur or Latur stands without calling anyone.

Reminders can go out through [WhatsApp automation](/services/whatsapp-automation/) on the official Business API. The manager decides when they are sent and how often.

## Where the documents live and who can see them

An Aadhaar card, a PAN card and a bank account number together are exactly what a fraudster looks for. On a personal phone, these photos get backed up to a private cloud account, forwarded by mistake, and carried along when that person changes jobs.

A proper system keeps every file on a secured server hosted in India, with **role-based access**. HR sees the full record. The accountant sees PAN and bank details. A branch supervisor sees only whether the file is complete. Each view and download is logged, and when a staff member leaves, their login is switched off and the documents stay with the company.

India's data protection law places duties on organisations that collect personal data, and an employer collecting staff documents should take that seriously. In general terms: collect only what you need, keep it safe, and do not hold it longer than required. We have written about [what the DPDP Act means for government software](/blog/dpdp-act-government-software/), and much of that thinking is useful for a private company too. Confirm with your CA or a legal advisor which documents you must keep and for how long.

## Documents ready for PF and payroll

The point of collecting all this is to pay people correctly and complete their registrations. Once an employee's checklist is complete, the details they typed can be checked by the manager against the uploaded documents and exported as one sheet: name, PAN, bank account number and IFSC, ready for the accountant or the payroll software.

The files are named consistently by employee, so when the CA asks for a PAN copy, a search finds it.

A typical source of trouble is a name spelt differently on the Aadhaar card, PAN card and bank account. The checklist lets the manager catch this at the time of joining, when it is easiest to correct. Which documents PF and other registrations need depends on your establishment, so confirm the list with your CA or PF consultant before you set up the checklist.

## Common questions

### Does the employee need to install an app?

No. The link opens in the browser on any smartphone, and uploads use the phone's own camera. If an employee has no smartphone, the manager can upload on their behalf from the office, and the record shows who did it.

### Is it safe to send the link on WhatsApp?

Yes, because the link carries no documents, only the address of that employee's upload page. The page can ask for a one-time code on the employee's mobile number before it opens, and the link can be closed once the checklist is complete.

### What about staff who joined earlier?

They can be added the same way. The manager uploads the staff list, each person gets a link, and the checklist shows the gaps in existing records. It is also a good occasion to move documents out of old chats into one place.

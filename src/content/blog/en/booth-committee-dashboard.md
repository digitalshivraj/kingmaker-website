It is eight in the morning at a party office in Solapur. The Vidhan Sabha in-charge has a review with the district president and one question to answer: how many booths have a working committee? The answer is spread across an Excel sheet last updated before the previous meeting, three WhatsApp groups and the memory of a few mandal presidents.

So the morning goes in phone calls. The numbers that come back are already a guess, and the booths with no committee at all are the ones nobody mentions.

## What the morning view should show

A Vidhan Sabha constituency typically has a few hundred booths. Nobody can hold that many in their head, so the first screen should sort them into four groups before you make a single call.

- Complete committees: booths where every post your party has defined has a name and a working mobile number against it.
- Gaps: booths where a committee exists but some posts are vacant, with the vacant posts named.
- Empty booths: booths with no booth head and no committee at all.
- Inactive booth heads: booth heads who have not sent an update or closed a task since a date you choose.

A count alone is not enough. Each number should open into a list grouped by mandal or circle, so you know exactly whom to call.

Pay most attention to the last item. A committee that exists only on paper is not a committee, and the **last active date** beside each booth head is often the most honest column on the screen.

## How the data comes from a booth head's phone

The dashboard is only as true as what booth heads put into it, and booth heads are not office staff. Typically they are farmers, shopkeepers and teachers with a low-cost Android phone and a weak network, whether in Jalgaon, Amravati or Kolhapur district. So the entry has to be **shorter than a WhatsApp message**, and it has to be in Marathi.

The order of work looks like this.

1. The office loads the booth list for the constituency and maps each booth to its mandal.
2. Each booth head gets a login tied to their mobile number and their booth.
3. The booth head adds committee members from the phone: name, post and mobile number.
4. The mandal president checks the entries for their booths and confirms them.
5. After that, the booth head sends short updates: meeting held, task done, issue seen.

If a booth head is not comfortable with the app, the mandal president can enter on their behalf, and the dashboard records who made the entry. Any [field app](/services/mobile-apps/) for this work must run on cheap phones and weak networks, with few fields and large buttons.

## Tasks for karyakartas that close with proof

Today a task goes out as a message in a WhatsApp group and comes back as a row of thumbs-up signs. A thumbs-up is not completion.

On a dashboard, every task has an owner, the booths it applies to, a due date and a status. The karyakarta marks it done with a short note or a photo. The in-charge sees, mandal by mandal, which booths have finished, which are pending and which have **not opened the task at all**.

Typical tasks are simple: hold a booth meeting, verify every committee member's phone number, confirm attendance for a programme, complete a round of house visits.

## Issues from the field, sorted by area

Booth heads hear things first: a [water supply complaint](/blog/mla-office-grievance-system/), a damaged road, a dispute between two karyakartas, a committee member who has gone quiet. Today this reaches the in-charge as a late-night phone call, or not at all.

The dashboard gives it a path. The booth head reports the issue with a category, one line of text and a photo if needed. It goes to the mandal president first. What cannot be settled there moves up to the Vidhan Sabha level with its history attached.

Each morning, the in-charge should see open issues by category and by mandal. One complaint is an incident. **Several similar complaints from one mandal** are a pattern worth raising with the district president while it is still small.

## Each level sees only its own area

A list of karyakartas with phone numbers is among the most sensitive things a party office holds. An Excel sheet forwarded once cannot be called back. So access should follow the organisation chart.

- A booth head sees their own booth.
- A mandal president sees the booths in their mandal.
- The Vidhan Sabha in-charge sees the whole constituency.
- The district president sees every constituency in the district.

Nobody sees sideways into another area. When a person changes post, their access changes with it. With **role-based access**, exports are limited to the roles that need them and there is a record of who viewed or downloaded what.

Personal data of karyakartas, and anything connected to voters, comes under [data protection law](/blog/dpdp-act-government-software/) and Election Commission rules. ## Common questions

### Will booth heads actually use an app?

Yes, provided the entry is shorter than a WhatsApp message and the screen is in Marathi. Start with one small ask: committee names and phone numbers. Add tasks and issue reporting once that habit is formed.

### Can we start from the Excel sheets we already have?

Yes, existing booth and committee sheets can be loaded as the starting point. Booth heads then correct their own entries from the phone and mandal presidents confirm them.

### Who can see our karyakarta data?

Only the people your organisation gives a role to, and only for their own area. Before signing with any vendor, ask in writing where the data is hosted and how you get a full export if you stop. Kingmaker Enterprises hosts client data in India.

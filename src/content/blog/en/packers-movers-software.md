A family in Pune is shifting to Nagpur next month. They call three packers and movers. Your call comes while you are standing beside a half-loaded truck in Nashik. You say you will send someone for a survey and write the name on the back of a challan book.

By evening the challan book is in the truck. A second enquiry has come through the website form to an email nobody opens, and a third came from an ad to a phone the office boy answers. Three customers, three places, no list. The family in Pune books with whoever called back first.

## Every enquiry in one list

A packers and movers company typically gets work from direct calls, Google and Meta ads, the website form and references from old customers.

The fix is not more staff. It is **one enquiry list** that every source feeds into, with the same fields each time:

- Customer name and phone number
- From city and to city, with the shifting date
- Type of move: household, office, vehicle or part load
- Where the enquiry came from
- Who in your team owns it, and the next follow-up date

Once the source is recorded against every enquiry, you can see which ads bring people who pick up the phone and which bring wrong numbers. That is the idea behind a [lead firewall that stops you paying for bad leads](/blog/lead-quality-stop-paying-for-bad-leads/).

## Survey and quotation on the same record

When the surveyor reaches the customer's flat, he opens the same enquiry on his phone instead of starting a fresh page. He lists the items room by room and notes the floor, the lift, how far the truck must park, and which items are fragile or need dismantling.

The quotation is then built from that list, not from memory. Packing, loading, transport, unloading and any insurance you offer appear as separate lines, with taxes as your CA advises. The customer gets it as a PDF on WhatsApp.

This ends a typical argument: the customer remembers one figure from a phone call and the supervisor mentions another at the door. With **one written quotation per job**, both sides look at the same paper. Each quotation also carries a status: sent, in follow-up, confirmed or lost, with the reason if it is lost.

## Job card, packing list and vehicle

When the customer confirms, the quotation becomes a job card. Nobody types the details again. The job card is what the supervisor, the packers and the driver all work from. It should carry:

1. Pickup and delivery addresses, dates and the customer's contact.
2. The packing list from the survey, with item counts and condition notes.
3. The team assigned: supervisor and packers.
4. The vehicle number, the driver's name and phone.
5. The amount agreed, the advance received and the balance due at delivery.

At loading, the supervisor ticks items off the packing list on his phone. On a part load, when one truck from Mumbai to Kolhapur carries goods for two families, each job has its own list and carton count, so nothing gets mixed at unloading.

Ask your CA or transport advisor which documents must travel with the vehicle for your kind of consignment, and attach them to the job card.

## Tracking and delivery confirmation with photos

Once the truck leaves, the customer has one question: where are my goods? Typically they call the owner, who calls the driver, who is on the highway and cannot answer.

A simpler way is a status on the job that the supervisor or driver changes with one tap: packed, loaded, in transit, reached the destination city, out for delivery, delivered. Each change can send the customer a short WhatsApp message. Send these through [WhatsApp automation](/services/whatsapp-automation/) on the official API. Our post on [doing WhatsApp the official way](/blog/whatsapp-business-api-vs-tools/) explains why.

The second habit is **photos at both ends**. At loading: the packed items and the closed truck. At delivery: the unloaded items and anything the customer points out. The customer checks the count against the packing list and confirms delivery on the supervisor's phone.

## Payments, dues and claims

Money in this business comes in pieces: an advance at booking, a balance at delivery, cash handed to the driver, UPI to the owner, and corporate clients who pay on credit.

Each job needs **its own small ledger**: amount agreed, amount received, by which mode, collected by whom, and what is still due. Cash collected by a driver should show as pending until it reaches the office. For corporate clients, a dues list sorted by age tells you whom to call first.

Claims belong on the same record. When a customer reports damage, the claim opens against the job, with the survey's condition notes and the loading and delivery photos already attached. What your transit insurance covers depends on the policy, so confirm the terms with your insurer or a legal advisor before promising anything in a quotation.

## Common questions

### Can a company with one office use a system like this?

Yes. The flow from enquiry to delivery is the same for one office or ten. Start with the enquiry list and quotations, then add job cards, tracking and payments once the staff are comfortable.

### Will drivers and packers really use it on their phones?

They will, if their screen asks only for what they already do: tick items, change a status, take photos. Marathi labels and large buttons matter more than extra features.

### Who owns the customer and job data?

You do, and you should be able to export it whenever you want. Before buying any software, ask where the data is hosted, who can see it and how you take it out. Kingmaker hosts data in India, and Mahima One runs on the client's own server.

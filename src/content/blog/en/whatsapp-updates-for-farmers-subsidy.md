A farmer in Jalna district hands his documents to the sprinkler dealer and goes home. From the next morning he calls. "Saheb, where is my file?" The counter staff stop what they are doing, find the file, and repeat what they said yesterday.

The farmer is not being difficult. He has put his own money and his land papers into a process he cannot see, and the phone call is his only window into it. Give him a better window and he has less reason to call.

## Why the farmer keeps calling

A subsidy application passes through several desks: the dealer, the online portal, the department's officers, the bank. It can wait at each one. From the farmer's side, waiting and being forgotten look exactly the same.

So he calls the one person in the chain whose number he has. For a farmer producer company (FPC) handling applications for its members, the calls land on whoever opened the file.

Typically, those calls ask one of three things:

- Where is my file now?
- Is anything pending from my side?
- What happens next?

A short message at each stage answers all three before he asks.

## One short Marathi message at each stage

Stages differ from scheme to scheme, so confirm the exact steps for yours with the agriculture department office. A typical micro-irrigation file moves through something like the six below. Write one message for each, in the plain Marathi your counter staff speak. Here is what each one says, in English:

1. Documents received. "Namaskar {name}, we have received all your documents. Next step: filling the online application."
2. Application submitted. "Your application has been filed online. Application number {number}. We are now waiting for pre-sanction. Nothing is pending from your side."
3. Pre-sanction received. "Your application has received pre-sanction. We will call you to fix the installation date."
4. Installation done. "The set has been installed in your field. The officer's field inspection is now pending. We will tell you the date as soon as we know it."
5. Inspection done, bill uploaded. "The field inspection is complete. The bill and photos are uploaded on the portal. Your file has moved forward for the subsidy."
6. Subsidy credited. "The portal shows your subsidy as credited. Please check your bank passbook."

Keep one more ready for a file that is stuck on the farmer's side: "One document is missing from your file: {document}. Send a photo of it to this number or bring it to the shop."

Each message says **where the file is, what is pending and what comes next**. Do not promise a date for the money. That decision belongs to the department, and a missed date brings back the calls with more anger.

The messages are only as reliable as the register behind them. If the stage lives in a notebook, someone has to remember to send each update. ## Voice notes for farmers who prefer listening

Many farmers use WhatsApp every day but would rather listen than read. Older farmers in particular will forward a text to a son in Pune and wait for him to call back and explain it.

The fix is simple. Put **a listen button** marked "Listen" in Marathi under the text. The farmer taps it and receives a short voice note for that stage.

A few habits make voice notes work:

- Record one note per stage, in a voice the farmer knows, ideally the owner or the counter staff.
- Speak the way people speak in your district, not in government Marathi.
- Say the same three things as the text: where the file is, what is pending, what comes next.
- Keep it about as long as reading the text aloud.

The text stays as the record. The voice note is for understanding.

## How often is too often

The rule that works is **one message per stage change**. If nothing has moved, send nothing. A daily "your file is in process" teaches the farmer to ignore the number, and then he misses the one message that needs him to act.

Some limits worth setting:

- If a file sits at one stage for a long stretch, send a single holding message saying it is with the department and nothing is pending from his side.
- Send during the day, not late at night.
- Keep offers and new product news out of the status thread. A farmer who agreed to file updates did not agree to advertisements.
- Let him stop the messages by replying with one word, such as "stop" in Marathi.

## Use an official business number and take opt-in first

Updates sent from a staff member's personal phone work until that person leaves, the phone breaks, or WhatsApp restricts the number for bulk sending. Then every farmer's thread is gone.

An official business number on the WhatsApp Business API shows your shop or FPC name, stays with the organisation, and sends message formats that WhatsApp has approved in advance. We have compared the options in [WhatsApp Business API versus tools](/blog/whatsapp-business-api-vs-tools/).

Opt-in is the other half. Take **recorded consent** at the counter when the file is opened:

1. Ask which number in the family has WhatsApp. It is often a son's or daughter's.
2. Note the farmer's agreement on the file form, or have him reply "yes" in Marathi to the first message.
3. Tell him what he will get: updates on this file, nothing else.

Data protection rules in India expect consent for this kind of contact. Confirm the details for your organisation with a legal advisor.

## Common questions

### Can we send these updates from the owner's personal WhatsApp?

You can, but it will not hold up as files grow. A personal number has no approved templates, no record of consent, and can be restricted for bulk messaging. An official business number avoids all three problems.

### What if the farmer does not use WhatsApp himself?

Send the updates to the family member he names, and record that it was his choice. Add the voice note so the message can be played to him at home. Keep the shop phone open for those who still prefer to call.

### Should the message mention the subsidy amount?

Only repeat what the portal or the sanction letter shows. Do not estimate an amount or a credit date, because the department decides both. If the farmer asks, tell him where the figure will appear.

In a hardware shop in Jalgaon, the first week of every month goes the same way. The owner downloads the bank statement, prints it, and writes a party name in pen beside each line. Then he photographs the pages and sends them to the CA on WhatsApp, with a page of notes on cash received.

A few days later the CA's assistant calls. Who sent this ₹18,500 by NEFT? Was this UPI entry a customer or a personal transfer? The owner checks his diary, guesses at two or three, and the assistant types every line into Tally by hand.

## Why the same payment gets written three times

The bank statement knows the date and the amount. It does not know which customer sent the money, or which bill it was for. That knowledge sits in the owner's head and in a diary on the counter.

So the month-end ritual is really a transfer of memory. The owner writes it in the diary, then on the statement, and the CA's office types it a third time. Each copy is a chance for a wrong party or a missed entry.

Typical results of this routine:

- Entries parked in a suspense account because nobody could say whose money it was.
- A customer who insists he has paid, and no quick way to check.
- Books that are ready only when a return is due, so the owner never sees them in between.

The cause is simple. **The information is captured at the wrong time**, weeks after the money moved.

## Record each payment and receipt once, against a party

The fix is to make the entry **on the day the money moves**, on a phone, and never write it again.

In a payment register built for the shop, it goes like this. A customer pays at the counter by UPI. The person at the till opens the register, picks the party and saves the receipt. A supplier is paid by cheque in the evening. Same screen, marked as a payment.

Each entry carries only what the CA will need later:

1. The party, chosen from a list so the spelling never changes.
2. Whether it is a receipt or a payment.
3. The amount and the date.
4. The mode: cash, UPI, cheque or bank transfer.
5. The bill number or a short note, if there is one.

Staff can add entries but not edit old ones. The owner sees everything.

## Bank entries matched to parties

At the end of the month the owner downloads the statement from net banking as a file and uploads it.

The system compares each bank line with the entries already recorded. Where the amount, date and reference agree, the line is marked as matched and the party is already attached. **What remains is a short list of unmatched lines**: a bank charge, a transfer nobody recorded, a customer who paid without informing the shop.

For those, the system suggests a party from the narration, such as a name in the UPI reference, and the owner confirms or corrects it. This is the principle in our [AI automation work](/services/ai-automation/): the software proposes, a person approves.

Cash entries have no bank line. They stay in the register and go to the CA as cash vouchers.

## An export your CA imports into Tally

Once the month is matched, the owner sends the CA one file instead of a set of photographs.

Tally accepts vouchers imported from a file in a set format. The export puts every receipt and payment into that format, with party names mapped to the ledger names in the CA's Tally. That mapping is done once at the start, with the CA's office, because an import works cleanly only when the names agree.

The CA still reviews the entries before importing. GST and income tax treatment, and the head each expense belongs to, remain the CA's decisions. Confirm with your CA which Tally version and import format they use before anything is built.

What changes for the CA's office is the typing. The assistant checks a file instead of keying in a statement line by line. We apply the same idea to other [ledgers and exports](/services/data-document-systems/) a business keeps.

## A party-wise ledger and dues report for the owner

The export helps the CA. The ledger helps the owner, on any day of the month.

Because every entry sits against a party, the register can show:

- A party-wise ledger: every receipt and payment for one customer or supplier, in date order.
- A dues report: who owes money, how much, and since when.
- The day's collection, split by cash and bank.

Dues are only as correct as the starting figures, so opening balances come from the CA's books at the start and bills are entered as sales happen.

Think of a cloth trader in Solapur calling a retailer for payment. With the diary, the call starts with an argument about what was paid in Diwali week. With the ledger open on the phone, the trader reads out dates and amounts, or sends the statement as a PDF on WhatsApp.

## Common questions

### Will my CA have to change how they work in Tally?

No, the CA keeps using Tally as before and adds one step: importing the file after reviewing it. Party names are mapped to the CA's existing ledgers, so their chart of accounts stays as it is.

### Does this replace Tally or my CA?

No, it replaces the retyping. The register captures payments and receipts at the shop, and the CA's Tally stays the book of account for returns and finalisation. Tax treatment and compliance questions stay with your CA.

### Do I have to share my net banking password?

No, you download the statement from your bank yourself and upload the file, so the software never needs your login. Do not share net banking credentials with any software vendor.

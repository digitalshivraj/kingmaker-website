A farmer in Jalna district applies on MahaDBT for a sprinkler set. His neighbour, who applied in the same season, has the subsidy in the bank. He is still asking the dealer what happened to his file.

In a typical case like this, nothing big went wrong. The file is stuck on something small: a spelling, a blurred photo, a message nobody read.

## What MahaDBT does for a farmer

MahaDBT is the Maharashtra government's direct benefit transfer portal. For farmers, it puts many agriculture department schemes behind one login, such as farm implements, drip and sprinkler irrigation and horticulture. The farmer registers, fills in personal and land details, and applies for the items needed.

A file then usually moves through stages: application, selection, document upload, scrutiny, pre-sanction, purchase, bill upload, inspection where the scheme asks for it, and payment into the farmer's bank account. The exact stages differ by scheme, so treat this as a general picture.

**Every stage is a place where a file can wait.** PoCRA has its own steps, covered in our post on [tracking PoCRA subsidy applications from camp to credit](/blog/pocra-subsidy-application-tracking/).

## Names and bank details that do not match

The Aadhaar card has one spelling. The land record has the father's name in a different order. The bank passbook uses initials. A person sees the same farmer. A system that matches records does not.

Typical problems:

- The name is spelled differently on Aadhaar, the land record and the bank account.
- The bank account is not linked with Aadhaar, or has gone inactive.
- The mobile number linked with Aadhaar is an old one, so the OTP goes to a phone nobody has.

The subsidy comes by direct transfer, so a bank problem can hold up money even after everything else is approved. **Correct the record at its source** before applying: the Aadhaar centre for Aadhaar, the bank branch for linking, the revenue office for land records.

## Documents that are missing or cannot be read

After selection, the portal asks for uploads. Land records, a quotation and later the purchase bill are commonly asked for, and some categories need a caste certificate. The list depends on the scheme, so read it on the portal.

The usual mistakes are simple: a photo taken in dim light, a page with one corner cut off, an old land extract, the right document in the wrong slot, an upload that failed on a weak network.

When the scrutiny officer cannot read a document, the file is sent back and waits until someone uploads it again. So photograph each paper flat, in daylight, and **open every uploaded file once to check it**. For irrigation schemes, our list of [documents to keep ready for drip and sprinkler subsidy](/blog/drip-sprinkler-subsidy-documents-maharashtra/) is a good starting point.

## Selection is a deadline, not a result

Many farmers relax when the selection message comes. But selection only opens a window. The portal shows a last date for uploading documents, and a selection that is not acted on can lapse.

After that, the first mistake is buying too early. A dealer in Solapur offers a good price, the farmer buys, and the bill is dated before the pre-sanction. Such a purchase can be refused for subsidy.

The second is waiting too long, until the date for purchase and bill upload has passed.

The rule of thumb: **buy nothing before pre-sanction, and do not sit idle after it.** Dealers should check that the bill matches the sanctioned item and the farmer's name in the application.

## Messages nobody reads, status nobody checks

The portal sends messages to the registered mobile number. Often that number belongs to a son working in Pune, or to the CSC operator who filled the form. The farmer never sees the message.

Meanwhile the status on the portal changes quietly. From the farm, a file that was sent back looks the same as a file still in process.

Three habits help:

1. Register a mobile number that someone in the family reads every day.
2. Log in and check the status regularly, and after every message.
3. Write down the application number and each last date the portal shows.

A CSC operator or dealer who filed many applications at once needs a list, not memory. ## Confirm every step on the official portal

Scheme rules change. The department revises eligible items, subsidy amounts, the selection method, document lists and dates. Last year's experience and a forwarded WhatsApp message are not reliable guides.

Before you act, check the official MahaDBT portal and the notices on it. When something is unclear, ask the taluka agriculture office or your agriculture assistant, and carry the application number. **Treat this post as a checklist of habits, not as the rule book.**

Do not share your password or OTP with a stranger, and remember that nobody outside the department can promise a selection or a sanction.

## Common questions

### Can I correct a mistake after submitting the application?

Sometimes, depending on the scheme and the stage the file has reached. When scrutiny sends a file back, the portal generally lets you upload the document again. A wrong name or bank detail has to be corrected with Aadhaar, the bank or the revenue office first.

### Can I buy the equipment before pre-sanction?

No, you should wait. A purchase made before pre-sanction can be refused for subsidy. Confirm the current rule for your scheme with the agriculture office before paying the dealer.

### Who do I ask when the status has not moved?

Ask the taluka agriculture office first, with your application number in hand. The helpdesk details on the official portal are the other route. A dealer or CSC operator can read the status for you but cannot move the file.

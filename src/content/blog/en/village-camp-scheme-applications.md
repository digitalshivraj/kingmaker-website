A dealer in Hingoli, Buldhana or Solapur district arranges a camp at the Gram Panchayat office for [drip and sprinkler subsidy applications](/blog/drip-sprinkler-subsidy-documents-maharashtra/). Farmers arrive from early morning. Some carry every paper. Many carry half. By afternoon the dealer's assistant has a phone gallery full of Aadhaar cards and land extracts mixed with family photos, and a notebook with names in three different handwritings.

A few days later nobody can say which farmer still owes a bank passbook copy, whose photograph came out blurred, or which applications are ready to file. The camp happened, but the applications did not move. What fixes this is one phone, one checklist and one sheet at the end of the day.

## Before the camp: announce it and send the checklist

Most missing documents are decided before the camp begins. A farmer who hears only "come on Thursday for the scheme" will come with whatever is at hand.

Announce the camp where the village already looks: the Gram Panchayat notice board, the village WhatsApp groups and the dealer's counter. State the date, the place, the scheme and who should come.

Send the **document checklist in Marathi** along with the announcement, not after it. A typical checklist for an agriculture scheme:

- Aadhaar card of the applicant
- Land record extracts for the plot in the application
- Bank passbook, first page
- A mobile number the farmer personally answers
- Any scheme-specific paper, such as a quotation, where the scheme asks for one

The exact list differs by scheme. Confirm it with the taluka agriculture office before you print anything.

Also settle who brings what. The farmer brings originals. The organiser brings a charged phone, a power bank, printed checklists and blank slips. The Gram Panchayat staff arrange a room, a table near a window for light, and one person to manage the queue.

## At the camp: one farmer, one entry

Work on one farmer at a time, in the same order every time.

1. Register the farmer on the phone: name as on the Aadhaar card, village, mobile number and scheme.
2. Photograph each document in checklist order, flat on the table, in daylight. Check that the photo is readable before you move on.
3. Mark every checklist item as received or missing.
4. If something is missing, write it on a slip in Marathi and hand it to the farmer before they leave the table.
5. Return all originals before the next farmer sits down.

One rule holds the whole camp together: **an entry is not complete until every item is marked**, received or missing, never blank.

The photographs should attach to the farmer's entry, not sit loose in the camera roll. A field app or form does this, and a well-built one keeps working when the signal drops. That is how we build [field apps for cheap phones and weak networks](/services/mobile-apps/).

## Keep documents out of personal galleries

An Aadhaar card and a bank passbook page are the kind of papers that get misused. In a personal gallery they get backed up to a private cloud account, forwarded into WhatsApp groups, and stay on the phone when the assistant changes jobs.

A few habits prevent this:

- Capture documents inside the app or form, so each photo goes to the farmer's record on a secured server and not to the gallery.
- Give each person who processes applications their own login, and remove it when their work ends.
- Do not send documents over personal WhatsApp chats or groups.
- **Tell the farmer why** you are photographing the papers and who will see them.

Whoever collects these documents is responsible for them. India's data protection law puts duties on anyone holding personal data, and how it applies to your camp is a question for the department or a legal advisor. ## One sheet the organiser reviews that evening

When the camp closes, the organiser should be looking at one sheet, not a gallery and a notebook. It shows:

- every farmer registered that day
- which applications are complete and ready to file
- which are incomplete, with the missing item against each name
- which photographs need to be retaken
- who made each entry

Review it the same evening, while the person who made the entries can still answer "what happened with this one?". Correct spellings and mobile numbers now. Then split the sheet in two: **complete applications go for filing**, incomplete ones go to follow-up.

## After the camp: work the follow-up list

The incomplete half of the evening sheet is your follow-up list. Each line has the farmer's name, mobile number, the missing document and who will chase it.

Call or message each farmer in Marathi and name the exact paper. "Your bank passbook first page is pending" gets a response. "Your documents are incomplete" usually does not.

Fix one collection point, usually the dealer's shop or the Gram Panchayat office, so farmers know where to bring the paper. When it arrives, photograph it into the same entry, mark it received, and the name drops off the list. The organiser can see who has not responded and may need a visit.

## Common questions

### What if the network is weak at the Gram Panchayat office?

The camp can still run if your app or form saves entries on the phone and uploads them when the signal returns. Test this at the venue before camp day.

### Does the farmer need a smartphone?

No, the farmer needs no smartphone at the camp, because registration and photographs happen on the organiser's phone. A basic phone number that the farmer answers is enough for follow-up calls.

### Who should see the farmers' documents after the camp?

Only the people who process and file the applications should see them. Give each of them a separate login rather than one shared password. How long to keep the documents after filing is a question for the department or a legal advisor.

A son working in Pune gets a call from home in Jalgaon district. His father's knee has got worse and the family wants to see an orthopaedic doctor this week. He searches on his phone at night, finds a hospital in the city and opens its website.

He sees a photo of the building, a paragraph about the hospital's mission and a landline number that nobody answers after OPD hours. Nothing tells him whether the orthopaedic doctor sits on Thursday. He goes back and opens the next result.

## The four questions a patient brings to your website

Patients and their relatives do not read a hospital website. They check it. Typically they arrive with four questions and leave as soon as these are answered, or as soon as they see they will not be.

1. Which doctor? Is there a specialist for this problem, and what is the doctor's name?
2. Which days? When is the OPD, and does the doctor sit every day or only on some days?
3. Which tests? Can the sonography, X-ray or blood test be done here, or will we be sent elsewhere?
4. How do we reach? Where exactly is the hospital, and which number do we call?

A good hospital website answers all four **within two taps from the home page**. Everything else on the site is secondary.

## Doctor pages with qualifications and OPD timings

Give every doctor a separate page. A single "Our team" page with ten small photos does not answer the first question.

Each doctor page should carry the full name, a clear photo, qualifications as registered, the speciality in plain words, the conditions the doctor commonly treats, languages spoken, and OPD days and timings. If a visiting consultant comes from Mumbai or Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar on fixed days, say which days.

Write the speciality the way patients say it. "Bone and joint doctor (orthopaedic surgeon)" helps the relative who does not know the medical term. And remember that **OPD timings must match the board at reception**. A patient who travels from another taluka on the wrong day will not come back.

## A booking link and a WhatsApp button on every page

Once the patient has found the doctor, the next step should be visible on the same screen. Put two buttons on every page, fixed at the bottom of the phone screen: book an appointment, and WhatsApp the hospital.

The booking button can open a simple form or a token system. ## Marathi and English versions, both complete

In a typical family, the person searching and the person deciding are different people. The son in Pune may read English. The mother in Nanded who decides which hospital to trust reads Marathi.

So the site needs both languages, and both must be complete. A Marathi version where only the menu is translated and the doctor pages stay in English is not a Marathi version. Doctor names, specialities, test names, timings and directions should all read naturally in Marathi, written by someone who speaks it, not translated word for word.

Put the language switch at the top of every page, and make it stay on the same page when the reader switches.

## Fast loading on a cheap phone

Test your website on the kind of phone your patients carry: a low-cost Android handset on mobile data, not the office Wi-Fi.

These are the things that usually slow a hospital site down:

- A sliding banner of large photos on the home page.
- A video that plays by itself.
- Timings and doctor lists uploaded as images or PDFs, which are heavy on mobile data and hard for search engines to read properly.
- Pop-ups that cover the phone number.

Write timings, tests and addresses as plain text. Compress photos. A page that opens quickly on a weak network in Amravati or Solapur district will open quickly everywhere.

## Pages that help you show up in Google and AI answers

People search for a problem and a place: "skin doctor in Kolhapur", "sonography centre near me". Some now ask an AI assistant the same question in a full sentence. Both can only recommend what they can read.

That means one page per doctor, one page per department, and a tests page listing each investigation by name. Add a contact page with the full address, a map, landmarks and parking, written as text. Keep the hospital's name, address and phone number identical on the website and on your [Google Business Profile](/blog/google-business-profile-for-doctors/). ## Common questions

### Does a hospital need a website if it already has a Google Business Profile?

Yes, because the two do different jobs. The profile shows your name, phone number and location on the map. The website holds what the profile cannot: a page for each doctor, the list of tests and a booking link.

### Who should update OPD timings on the website?

Someone inside the hospital should, usually the administrator or the reception in-charge. Ask for a site where timings and doctor leave can be changed from a phone without calling the developer. A website with old timings does more harm than no website.

### What can a hospital say about its doctors on its website?

Stick to facts: registered qualifications, speciality, experience and OPD timings. Professional conduct rules place limits on how doctors and hospitals may advertise, so avoid claims about cures or comparisons with other hospitals. Confirm the wording with your state medical council or a legal advisor.

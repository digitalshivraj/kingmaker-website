A class teacher in a Zilla Parishad school in Solapur district fills in a sheet for the ID card vendor: name, class, division, roll number, parent's mobile number. A photographer visits once, the children line up against a wall, and the sheet and the photos travel to the printer separately.

When the cards come back, one surname is spelt two ways in the same class, some photos are too blurred to use, and a Class 7 student is holding a Class 6 card. The correction list goes back on WhatsApp and the round starts again.

## Where the errors come from

Almost every wrong card can be traced to one of three places.

- Spellings: a Marathi name written by hand is typed into English by someone who has never met the child. Dnyaneshwar becomes Gyaneshwar, Shaikh becomes Sheikh, and the father's name is shortened or dropped.
- Photos: taken in poor light, cropped from a group picture, or saved under a file name that nobody can match to a student later.
- Class and roll number: last year's list is reused, so promoted students carry the old class, and two children in one division share a roll number.

The common thread is **retyping**. Each time the data moves from paper to Excel to the printer's computer, a person types it again and a new error can enter. The fix is to enter each student once, at the school, and never type that record again.

## Collect data and photos on a phone, school by school

The person who knows the child should enter the child. That means the class teacher, phone in hand, in the classroom.

A workable routine looks like this:

1. The education office or the printer creates a login for each school.
2. The teacher picks the class and division, then adds each student: name in Marathi and English, roll number, date of birth, parent's mobile number and anything else the card design needs.
3. The photo is taken from the same screen, against a plain wall, and **attached to that student's record** at once. There are no file names to match later.
4. The teacher reads the name back against the school's admission register before saving.
5. The headmaster reviews each class list and marks the school as submitted.

Teachers use their own phones and many schools have a weak network, so the app has to work on low-cost handsets and patchy data. ## Checks to run before anything is printed

Once a card is printed, a mistake costs money and goodwill. So the checks happen while the data is still on screen.

- Missing photos: a list, class by class, of students with no photo or a photo too dark or small to print.
- Duplicates: the same student entered twice, or two students in one division with the same roll number.
- Name mismatches: the English name does not match the Marathi name, or siblings in the same school have their surname spelt differently.
- Blank fields: no date of birth, no class, no parent's mobile number.

A school should not move to printing until its list of open issues is empty, or the headmaster has approved each exception. Software can flag a probable mismatch, but **a person at the school approves the final list**.

## One PDF per school, ready to print

For a printer in Nagpur or Kolhapur, the worst input is a folder of loose photos and an Excel sheet. Someone at the press has to match each face to each row, and that is where a wrong photo lands on a right name.

With checked data, the system produces **one PDF for each school**. Every card is laid out in the approved design, in class, division and roll number order. The printer opens the file and prints. Nothing is typed at the press.

Because the file is already in class order, the cards come off the machine sorted and can be bundled class by class. A cover sheet with the school name and the card count for each class lets the headmaster tally the bundle on delivery. This kind of print pack is part of our [data and document systems](/services/data-document-systems/) work.

If a student joins late or loses a card, the school adds or reopens that one record and prints that one card.

## Tracking which schools are complete

An education officer handling a taluka or a district such as Nashik or Amravati does not need to open every student record. The officer needs to know which schools are stuck.

A simple status board answers that. For each school it shows students entered against enrolment, photos pending, issues open, and whether the school is submitted, printed or delivered. The follow-up call then goes only to the schools that have not started, and the printer knows which files are safe to print today.

## Common questions

### Can teachers use their own phones for this?

Yes. The collection screen should open on an ordinary Android phone with the school's login. Each teacher sees only that school's students, and the photo sits with the student's record, not in a WhatsApp group.

### What happens if a student is absent on the day photos are taken?

The record stays open with a missing-photo flag. The teacher adds the photo when the student returns, and the school is not marked complete until then, unless the headmaster decides to print the rest first.

### Who is responsible for checking spellings, the school or the printer?

The school. The class teacher checks each name against the admission register and the headmaster approves the list, so the printer receives a final file and prints it as it is.

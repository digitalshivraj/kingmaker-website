It is eleven at night at a lodge near the railway station in Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar. Two travellers from Germany put their passports on the counter. The night clerk knows the register entry for an Indian guest by heart. For a foreign guest he only remembers that there is "some form" the owner handles.

The owner is asleep. The clerk photographs the passports on his own phone, writes the names in the register and hopes somebody files the form tomorrow. That gap, between a guest arriving and the report reaching the authorities, is where trouble typically starts.

## What Form C is and why it exists

Form C is the report a hotel, lodge or homestay sends to the immigration authorities to say that a foreign national is staying on its premises. It exists so the government knows where a visitor is staying while in India. The same reasoning sits behind the ordinary register, covered in our post on [hotel guest records and what police ask lodges to keep](/blog/hotel-guest-records-police-requirements/).

Responsibility for the report rests with the person or business running the property, not with the guest.

Hotel owners who learnt this routine years ago should know that the old name is now mostly a habit: a 2025 law, the Immigration and Foreigners Act, governs the report today and gives it a different form number. Take the current form number from the portal or your registration office, not from memory.

## What the hotel collects from a foreign guest

The form on the portal is the final list. In general terms, it asks for:

- Identity: full name as printed in the passport, nationality, date of birth and a photograph.
- Passport: number, place and date of issue, date of expiry.
- Visa: number, type and validity.
- Arrival in India: date and place of entry, where the guest has come from and where they go next.
- Stay: check-in date and time, expected length of stay and purpose of visit.
- Contact: phone number and home address abroad.

Ask for the **original passport and visa**, not a picture on the guest's phone.

Recent guides for hoteliers also count Overseas Citizen of India cardholders among the guests to be reported. Ask your registration office how this applies to you.

A passport copy is personal data. It belongs on the hotel's own system, not in a clerk's photo gallery. ## Filing on the official portal within the time the rules set

The government's FRRO portal, [indianfrro.gov.in](https://indianfrro.gov.in/), is where the report goes. First-time users create an account there under the accommodation provider category, and the same login is then used for every later guest.

The clock for this report starts when the guest arrives, and the filing is electronic. The rules fix a time limit from that point. Read the exact limit on the portal or ask your registration office.

The safest habit is simple: **file at check-in**, not later.

## A front-desk routine for a busy night

1. Ask every guest for photo identity. A foreign passport starts this routine.
2. See the original passport and visa. Scan or photograph them on the hotel's device.
3. Make the entry in the hotel's own guest register.
4. Log in to the portal and file the report before the guest leaves the counter.
5. Save whatever confirmation the portal shows against the register entry.
6. At check-out, write the departure in the register. Departure belongs in the report too, so the entry is not finished at check-in.

At least two people should know this routine and hold the login. A lodge in Nashik or Kolhapur cannot depend on the owner being awake.

## Keeping a record the police can check

Filing is half the job. The other half is showing an officer who stayed, when, and that it was reported.

For each foreign guest, keep four things together: the register entry, the passport and visa copy, the portal confirmation and the departure entry. With a paper register these end up in **three different places**: the book, a file of photocopies and somebody's phone.

A digital register keeps them in one entry, which is one reason [every lodge register in Maharashtra should be digital](/blog/digital-lodge-register-maharashtra-police/).

## Confirm the details with your FRRO or district police

The law is new and local practice differs. Large cities such as Mumbai have a Foreigners Regional Registration Office. In most districts the registration officer usually sits in the district police office, so a lodge in Nanded or Amravati can start there.

Ask once, and write the answers down:

- What is the current form name and the exact time limit?
- Must departure be reported on the portal as well?
- Are Overseas Citizen of India cardholders to be reported?
- How long must the hotel keep its copies?

For anything still unclear, ask a legal advisor.

## Common questions

### Does a small lodge or homestay also have to file Form C?

Yes. Size gives no exemption in the guidance published for hoteliers, which treats a single let-out room the same way as a large hotel. Confirm your own category with the registration office.

### What should the night clerk do if a foreign guest arrives late?

He should file the report at check-in itself, whatever the hour. The time limit runs from arrival, so waiting for the owner only uses up the margin. Give the night shift the login and the written routine.

### Is the hotel's own register enough for a foreign guest?

No, the register and Form C are two separate duties. The register is the hotel's record for the local police, and Form C is a report to the immigration authorities. A foreign guest needs both.

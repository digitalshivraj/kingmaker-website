A citizen in Solapur district writes to the tehsil office about a correction in a record. The letter is stamped at the inward desk. Two weeks later she comes in person and a clerk notes it on a slip. Then her son phones the office, and finally sends a photo of the letter to an officer's WhatsApp.

That is one complaint sitting in four places, and none of them knows about the others. When the head of office asks how many grievances are pending, nobody can give a number they trust.

## Complaints arrive through four doors

A tehsil or municipal office does not choose how citizens reach it. In a typical office, each channel has its own habit.

- Post: the letter is entered in the inward register and marked to a section, where it waits in a file.
- In person: the citizen meets whoever is at the desk, and the matter is noted on a slip or not at all.
- Phone: a promise is made on the call and nothing is written down.
- WhatsApp: the message stays on an officer's personal phone, even after the officer is transferred.

The channels are not the problem. Citizens will use whichever is easiest, and an office should not close any of these doors. The problem is that the four never meet in one place. ## One entry with a number, a department and an officer

The rule: **one complaint, one number**, whichever door it came through.

1. The clerk records the citizen's name, mobile number, subject and channel on one entry screen.
2. A paper letter is scanned or photographed and attached.
3. The clerk selects the department and desk the matter belongs to.
4. A responsible officer is named. A person, not "concerned section".
5. The system gives the grievance a number, and the citizen is told that number.

When the same citizen calls or visits again, the clerk searches by mobile number, finds the existing entry and adds a note. No second complaint is created. The entry becomes the full history: who received it, who holds it, what was done and when.

Record only what the office needs to resolve the matter. ## Follow-up dates and escalation when a date is missed

An entry without a date is just a better-looking file. Every open grievance carries a next follow-up date, set by the officer or by the office's own norms.

Each officer sees a short list: due today, overdue and newly assigned. After acting, the officer writes a brief note and either sets the next date or closes the grievance with a remark and the reference of the reply sent.

If a date passes with no update, the entry appears on the supervisor's list. If it stays untouched, it reaches the head of office's screen. The office decides the levels and the gaps between them.

For some services, time limits are laid down in government rules. Confirm the limits that apply to your office with the department and set the dates to match.

Escalation is not a punishment. It makes sure **a missed date is seen by someone** who can ask why.

## Updates to the citizen on WhatsApp or SMS

A common reason for a repeat visit is simply to ask what happened. A short message at three points answers that question before it is asked.

- On registration: the grievance number, the department and the desk handling it.
- On a change of status: for example, forwarded to another department or a site visit planned.
- On closure: the closing remark and where to collect any document.

Messages go in Marathi, on WhatsApp where the citizen uses it and by SMS where they do not. Keep them short and leave personal details out of the text.

## The monthly report: pending and closed by desk

The head of office needs **one page for the monthly review**, not a stack of registers. For each desk it shows grievances received, closed and still pending, how old the pending ones are, and how many were escalated. A second table shows the same by channel.

Read it for patterns, not for blame. A desk in a municipal office in Jalgaon or Amravati district with a long pending list may be slow, or it may be carrying twice the load of the next desk.

The report exports to a sheet or PDF for the review file.

## Common questions

### Do we have to stop accepting complaints on paper or by phone?

No. Citizens keep using post, the counter, the phone and WhatsApp as they do now. What changes is inside the office: the clerk makes one entry for each complaint, and a paper letter is scanned and attached to it.

### Who can see a complaint inside the office?

Only the people who work on it. Access is role-based, so the entry clerk, the responsible officer, the supervisor and the head of office each see what their role needs.

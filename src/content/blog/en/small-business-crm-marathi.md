It is admission season at a coaching institute in Latur. Enquiries come by phone, at the counter, from Instagram and from parents who heard about the institute from a neighbour. One counsellor writes them in a notebook. The other saves numbers in her phone contacts.

The owner paid for a CRM last year. It is in English, the first screen is a long form, and nobody has opened it since the training session. So every evening the owner still asks the same question: how many enquiries came today, and who called them back?

## Why staff do not use English CRMs

Most CRMs are designed for a sales team that sits at laptops and thinks in words like pipeline, opportunity and deal stage. A counter assistant at a tractor dealership in Jalgaon or a receptionist at a clinic in Solapur thinks in other, Marathi words: enquiry, call again, will visit, said no.

The typical reasons a CRM goes unused in a small business are simple.

- Language: every label is in English, so staff guess what a field means or skip it.
- Too many fields: the form will not save without an email, a company name and a deal value that nobody at the counter knows.
- Built for a laptop: the staff have phones, and the screen does not fit one.
- No benefit to the person typing: the entry helps the owner's report, but the notebook is faster for the person holding the pen.

None of this is laziness. **A register that takes longer than a notebook will lose to the notebook.**

## One lead register with source, follow-up date and status

The fix is not a bigger CRM. It is **one register that every enquiry enters**, whichever way it arrived, with the fewest fields that still answer the owner's questions.

- Name and mobile number. These two are enough to save the lead.
- What they asked about: the course, the model, the service.
- Source: walk-in, phone call, website, Google, Facebook or Instagram, reference.
- Follow-up date: the day someone has promised to call back.
- Status: a short list in the staff's own words, such as new, contacted, visit planned, converted, not interested.
- Handled by: the staff member responsible.

Everything after the name and number is a tap, not typing. If the same mobile number is entered twice, the register shows the existing lead instead of creating a second one.

Source deserves care. It tells you whether an enquiry came from a reference, an advertisement or [the website your business runs](/blog/small-business-website-maharashtra/), and that decides where the next round of spending goes.

## Follow-up reminders on the phone

The follow-up date is the field that earns money. In a typical small business, a lead is lost not because the customer said no, but because **nobody called back on the promised day**.

So the register should open each morning on one screen: today's follow-ups for that staff member. Each row shows the name, what they asked about and the last note. After the call there are two choices: set the next follow-up date, or close the lead with a status.

A missed follow-up does not disappear. It stays at the top of the list, marked overdue, and the owner can see it too.

## WhatsApp from inside the lead

Most calls end with the same request: send the details on WhatsApp. Without a system, that means saving the number, opening WhatsApp, finding the brochure in the gallery and typing a message.

With a WhatsApp button on the lead, the chat opens with the number filled in and **a ready Marathi message**: the fee structure, the price list, the location. The register notes that the message was sent, so the next person who opens the lead knows what the customer already has.

There are two ways to do this. The simple way opens WhatsApp on the staff member's own phone. The second sends from the business number through the official API, which also allows automatic messages such as a reminder before a planned visit. Meta has its own rules on message templates and customer consent, so confirm the current rules before sending anything in bulk.

## A daily report for the owner

The owner should not have to open the register to know how the day went. Every evening, a short summary reaches the owner's phone.

1. New enquiries today, split by source.
2. Follow-ups due, done and missed, for each staff member.
3. Leads converted and leads lost, with the reason.
4. Open leads that have no follow-up date at all.

Read across a season, the same report shows **which source brings customers who actually buy**. If you run [Google or Meta campaigns](/services/digital-marketing/), that is the number to judge them on, and it is how you [stop paying for leads that never answer the phone](/blog/lead-quality-stop-paying-for-bad-leads/).

## Common questions

### Can staff use it in Marathi while the owner reads in English?

Yes. Each user chooses a language, and the same lead appears with Marathi or English labels. Names and notes stay exactly as they were typed.

### Can we bring in old leads from Excel or a notebook?

Yes, an Excel sheet can be imported once its columns are matched to the register's fields. Notebook entries have to be typed in, so it usually makes sense to enter only the enquiries that are still open and start fresh from there.

### Do we need the WhatsApp Business API for this?

No, the register works without it. The simple version opens WhatsApp on the staff member's phone with the message ready. The API is needed only when messages must go out automatically from the business number, and it carries its own charges and Meta rules.

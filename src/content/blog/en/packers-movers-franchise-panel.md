A customer in Pune calls the head office of a packers and movers company. Her household goods were booked through the Nagpur franchise and she wants to know where the truck is. The head office has no record of the booking. Someone calls Nagpur, the franchise owner checks a diary, and the customer waits on hold.

This is typical of a company that has grown from one office to several. Each branch runs its own book: its own quotation pad, its own Excel sheet, its own cash register. The head office carries the brand name and the complaints, but it sees the numbers only when a branch chooses to send them.

## What the head office cannot see today

When every branch keeps its own book, the head office cannot answer simple questions.

- How many enquiries came in at each branch, and how many became bookings?
- Was the price quoted the company's rate or the branch manager's own?
- Which consignments are on the road, and which are late?
- How much cash has been collected, and how much has reached the bank?

None of this is hidden on purpose. It sits in registers, phone galleries and WhatsApp chats. The owner hears of a problem when a customer complains or a franchise disputes its commission at month end.

A franchise panel fixes this by making **one shared record** the place where the work is done, not a report typed up afterwards.

## Bookings, quotations, consignments and payments in one panel

The panel should follow a move in the order it happens.

1. Enquiry and quotation. The branch records the enquiry, the survey of goods and the price. The quotation goes out in the company's format, from the company's rate card.
2. Booking. When the customer agrees, the quotation becomes a booking with a date, an advance and a consignment number. Nobody types the details twice.
3. Consignment. Packing, loading, dispatch and delivery are marked against the same number, with the vehicle and the branch at each end.
4. Payment. Advance, balance and extra charges are entered against the booking, with who collected and how.

Because all four steps hang on one consignment number, the head office can open any move and see its full history. So can the branch receiving the goods at the other end.

If your invoices carry GST, ask your CA to confirm how each charge should be shown before the format is fixed.

## Franchise-level access and commission

A franchise owner is a business partner, not an employee. Owners use a panel only if they trust what it shows and what it hides.

So access is set by level. The head office sees every branch. A franchise sees **only its own** enquiries, bookings, customers and collections, plus consignments it must deliver for another branch. Inside the franchise, the owner decides what each staff member can open.

Commission is where disputes typically start. A typical move involves the branch that books and packs, the branch that unloads at the destination, and the head office that owns the brand. Worked out on paper at month end, each side arrives with a different total.

In a panel, the head office sets the commission rule for each franchise once. Every booking then shows the split, and the franchise sees the same figure the head office sees. Settlement becomes checking a list, not arguing over one.

## Tracking for the customer

A typical call to a packers and movers office is one question: where are my goods?

The customer should get the consignment number at booking and a link to check the status herself. For a household move, simple steps are enough: packed, loaded, dispatched, reached the destination city, delivered. Staff mark each step from a phone at the handover.

The same steps can go out as WhatsApp messages through the official API. Our page on [WhatsApp automation](/services/whatsapp-automation/) explains how this works in Marathi and English.

There is a second benefit. When the customer can see the status, the branch has a reason to keep it current, and the head office gets accurate **consignment status** without asking.

## Reports: branch performance, pending payments and claims

Once the work happens in the panel, reports need no extra typing. Three matter most to an owner.

- Branch performance: enquiries, quotations sent, bookings confirmed and revenue, branch by branch. A Nashik branch with many enquiries and few bookings needs a different conversation from a Kolhapur branch with few enquiries.
- Pending payments: which customers still owe a balance, which deliveries were completed without full payment, and which branch holds cash it has not deposited. This report protects the company's money.
- Claims: every damage or loss claim recorded against its consignment, with what was damaged, which branch packed it and where the claim stands. Over a season, **the claims report** shows whether the problem is one careless crew or one route. For transit insurance and liability, confirm the position with your insurer or a legal advisor.

A network with corporate relocation contracts or a courier arm may need extra reports, which is where [custom software](/services/custom-software/) work comes in.

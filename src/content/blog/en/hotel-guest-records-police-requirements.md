It is past eleven at a lodge near the bus stand in Nanded. A constable from the local station asks who stayed in room 6 last Tuesday. The clerk turns back the pages of the register and finds a name nobody can read, a phone number with nine digits and no note of which ID was shown.

Nobody at the lodge did anything dishonest. The entry was written in a hurry during a busy check-in. But the constable leaves without an answer, and the owner gets a call from the station the next morning.

## Why police ask for guest records

A lodge is where a person from outside the town sleeps for a night or two and then moves on. When police are looking for a missing person, a suspect in a theft or a fraud, or someone wanted in another district, the lodge register is one of the first places they look.

The register also protects the owner. If something goes wrong in a room, **a complete entry shows the lodge did its part**: it checked who the guest was and wrote it down at the time.

This is why police stations and district authorities in Maharashtra issue written instructions to lodges, hotels and guest houses from time to time. The instructions differ from place to place and they get revised. What applied in Kolhapur two years ago may not be what applies in Nashik today.

## What a guest entry usually contains

The exact list comes from your local order. An entry typically covers:

1. Full name, as written on the identity document.
2. The type of identity document shown and its number, checked against the original.
3. Permanent address.
4. Mobile number, checked with a missed call at the desk.
5. Purpose of the visit.
6. Names of everyone staying in the room, not only the person who pays.
7. Room number, with the date and time of check-in and check-out.
8. The guest's signature.

Foreign guests need more. A lodge that hosts a foreigner, an OCI cardholder included, answers to a separate central rule called Form III (earlier Form C): going by the [Bureau of Immigration page on hotel obligations](https://boi.gov.in/boi/contents/registration-authority/obligation-of-hotels-hospitals-educational-institutes), that record is electronic, stays on file for at least one year and has to be produced when the police or the registration officer ask.

## Keeping ID copies safely, and for a limited time

In a typical small lodge, photocopies of guest IDs sit in an open drawer, and photos of IDs sit in the receptionist's personal phone. An identity copy that reaches the wrong hands can be misused in the guest's name.

Three habits fix most of this:

- Take a copy only if your local order asks for one. If it asks only for the document type and number, write those down and hand the original back.
- Keep paper copies in a locked cupboard, not in the desk drawer.
- Fix a retention period, then shred or delete copies when it ends.

**Keep a copy only as long as you have a reason to**. Retention under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 follows the purpose: once the reason for holding a guest's details is over, the details are meant to go. The police order or a tax rule may set its own period, so confirm it with your police station, your CA or a legal advisor before you destroy anything. ## Sharing with the police station: paper register versus digital

With a paper register, the record lives at the front desk. The police see it when a constable visits, or when the lodge sends a list or photocopies to the station. Finding one guest means reading handwriting page by page, lodge by lodge.

With a **digital register**, the front desk types the entry on a phone or a computer. The software can refuse a nine-digit phone number or a blank ID field. The [station can see entries](/blog/lodge-register-pilot-sp-office/) as they are made and search by name, phone number or date. ## Confirm the current order with your local police station

This post describes common practice. It is **not the order for your town**. Before you change anything at the desk:

1. Ask your police station for a copy of the current written order for lodges.
2. Note the fields it lists, the identity documents it accepts and whether copies are required.
3. Ask how records are to be shared, and how long registers and ID copies are to be kept.
4. Ask in writing whether a digital register is accepted in place of the paper one.
5. Put the checklist at the front desk in Marathi.

## Common questions

### Is a handwritten register still enough?

It can be, where the local order asks for a register and does not say it must be digital. The entries still have to be complete and readable, so ask your police station what it accepts today.

### Can front desk staff photograph a guest's ID on their own phone?

It is better not to. A photo on a personal phone ends up in the gallery, in backups and in chat groups, and the lodge loses control of it when that employee leaves. Use a device the lodge owns.

### How long should a lodge keep guest records?

Keep them for the period your local police order states, and do not hold ID copies beyond that without a reason. Billing records may have their own period under tax rules, which your CA can confirm.

A citizen stands at the counter of a tahsil office in Jalgaon district, holding a photocopy of an application with an inward stamp on it. The clerk opens the inward register, finds the entry and reads the remark: marked to the establishment desk. That desk says it went up to the head of office. Nobody can say where it is today.

The register did its job. It proved the letter came in. What it cannot do is follow the letter after it leaves the inward table, and that is where the trouble usually starts.

## Where letters get lost between the two registers

Most offices keep two bound books. The inward register records what arrived: date, sender, subject and the desk it was marked to. The outward register records what was sent, under its own number. The pattern is much the same in Latur, Amravati or Ratnagiri.

**The gap is everything in between.** Typical weak points:

- The entry shows the first desk, not where the letter went after that.
- A reply goes out under an outward number, but nobody writes it against the inward entry, so the letter still looks pending.
- Handwriting, abbreviations and a new book every year make old entries hard to find.
- A reference from a higher office with a reply date looks the same on the page as a routine circular.

None of this is carelessness. A bound book holds one line per letter.

## What one entry should capture: number, scan and desk

In a digital register the inward clerk does the same work at the same table. The entry simply carries more.

1. **The system gives the number**, in the office's own series, so there are no gaps and no duplicates.
2. The clerk enters the sender, the subject, the sender's reference number and date, and the mode: hand, post or email.
3. The letter is scanned, or photographed on a phone, and attached to the entry.
4. The clerk selects the desk or officer it is marked to. That desk sees it in its own list once the entry is saved.
5. If a reply is needed, the clerk or superintendent sets a due date.

Outward entries work the same way, and each can be linked to the inward letter it answers. The inward entry then closes with the outward number recorded against it.

Scanned letters often carry names, addresses and identity details. Decide before you start who can open scans and who sees only the entry. Our plain-language note on [what the DPDP Act means for offices buying software](/blog/dpdp-act-government-software/) lists the questions to ask.

## Tracking which desk holds a file today

Every movement after the first marking is one step: forward to a desk with a short remark. The receiving desk acknowledges it. The entry keeps the full trail, so anyone with permission can see **which desk holds the letter today** and since which date.

For the head of office this becomes a simple daily view: how many letters each desk is holding, and which have been sitting longest. For the counter clerk, the citizen from the opening scene gets an answer from the screen instead of a walk through three rooms.

## Reminders for letters that need a reply

Reminders should come from the due date set at entry, not from someone's memory.

A workable pattern: the desk holding the letter is reminded before the due date, the superintendent gets a list of what is due this week, and **anything overdue rises to the top** of the head of office's screen.

Keep the list short. Set due dates only on letters that truly need a reply: references from higher offices, court matters, audit points and time-bound applications. Where a reply period is fixed by a rule, confirm it with your department before setting it in the system.

## Search in Marathi by number, subject or sender

A clerk should be able to type what they remember and find the letter. That means search by inward or outward number, subject, sender, the sender's reference number or a date range.

Subjects are usually written in Marathi, so **entry and search must work in Devanagari**, with English alongside for letters that arrive in English. The same sender often gets written three different ways. A pick list of frequent senders, such as the Collector office, the Zilla Parishad or the treasury, keeps spellings consistent and search reliable.

## Common questions

### Can we stop the paper register once the software is running?

Not until your department's rules allow it. A common approach is to run both side by side and print the digital register for the record room. Confirm this, and how long records must be kept, with your controlling office.

### Do we need new computers or a scanner?

Usually not. The register opens in a browser on the computers the office already has, and a phone camera can capture a letter where there is no scanner.

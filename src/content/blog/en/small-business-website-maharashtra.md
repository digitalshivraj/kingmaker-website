A customer in Kolhapur searches on her phone for a computer institute near her home. She taps the first website. A photo slider loads slowly, the text is in small English paragraphs, and she cannot find the fees, the batch timings or a phone number. She presses back and calls the next institute on the list.

The owner of the first institute paid for that website and wonders why it brings no enquiries. The design is rarely the problem. The site does not answer what a customer wants to know, in the language she reads, on the phone she owns.

## What a customer looks for first

A visitor to a small business website arrives with **three questions**. If the first screen answers them, she stays.

1. What you sell. One plain line at the top, such as "Tally and MS-CIT classes" or "AC repair and installation". Below it, the list of products or services, with prices or a price range where you can share one.
2. Where you are. City, area and a known landmark, a map link, opening hours and the weekly holiday. If you deliver or serve nearby talukas, say which ones.
3. How to contact you. A phone number that someone picks up, and a WhatsApp number.

The company history, awards and mission statement can go on an inner page. They are not why she came.

## Marathi and English, both on every page

Your customers speak to you in Marathi at the counter. Many of them also search in Marathi, or in Marathi typed in English letters. A website only in English makes a farmer in Latur or a parent in Jalgaon work harder than they should.

English still has its place. Company buyers, students and people who have recently moved to a city like Pune often search and read in English.

So **write every main page in both languages**, with a clear language switch at the top. Write the Marathi the way you speak it in the shop, not as a pasted machine translation. Products, prices, address and timings must match exactly in both versions.

## WhatsApp and call buttons on every page

A customer on a phone does not want to fill in a form and wait. She wants to tap once and talk to someone.

Keep **two buttons fixed at the bottom** of every page, where the thumb reaches: Call and WhatsApp. The call button should dial your number directly. The WhatsApp button should open a chat with a line already typed, such as "I want to know your catering rates", so you know what the enquiry is about.

Use a number the business owns, not a staff member's personal number. ## Speed on a cheap phone

Test your website the way your customer sees it: on a low-cost Android phone, on mobile data, standing in a market in Nanded or at a bus stand in Amravati. Not on the office Wi-Fi.

The usual causes of a slow site:

- Large photos uploaded straight from a camera.
- Sliders and autoplay videos at the top of the page.
- Pop-ups and extra widgets that load before the content.
- Text placed inside images, which is also harder for Google to read.

A **light page with real text** opens quickly and uses less of the customer's data. Keep your photos, but compress them, and type your words as text.

## Your Google Business Profile and reviews

When someone searches "tiles shop near me", the map results with star ratings often appear above the websites. That listing is your Google Business Profile. Your website and your profile should point to each other and say the same thing.

- The same name, address, phone number and hours in both places.
- Your website link added to the profile.
- A map link and a "Review us on Google" link on the website.
- A polite reply to every review, good or bad.

Ask for **reviews from real customers**, at the counter, when the work is done. Never buy reviews or write them yourself. Our guide to [Google Business Profile for doctors](/blog/google-business-profile-for-doctors/) is written for clinics, but the same steps work for a shop or an institute.

## Pages that answer common questions

Every business answers the same handful of questions on the phone all day. Do you deliver to Solapur? Do you accept UPI? When does the next batch start? Which documents are needed for admission?

Put each answer on the website in plain words. The best place is **a short page for each service**, with its own questions answered on it. This saves you calls, and it matches how people search: they type or speak full questions.

## Common questions

### Is a Google Business Profile enough without a website?

No, the profile gets you found but cannot explain your business in full. It shows your name, hours, photos and reviews, while the website holds the price list, service details and answers to questions. Use both and link them to each other.

### Should the website be in Marathi or in English?

Both, on every main page. Marathi serves the customers who speak to you in Marathi every day, and English serves company buyers and people new to the city. A language switch at the top lets each visitor choose.

### Does a small shop need online payment on its website?

Not at the start, for most shops and service businesses. Customers usually want to call or WhatsApp first, confirm the price and then pay by UPI or at the counter. Add online ordering later if customers start asking for it.
